Breast reconstruction

1. RT CHARM: 3-week vs 5-week postmastectomy radiation in patients with reconstruction

[Clinical trial] [United States and Canada]
Phase 3 randomized noninferiority trial (Alliance A221505, NCT03414970). Its results were presented at ASTRO 2024; what is new in this period is the full publication, online in the Journal of Clinical Oncology on October 12, 2026. It enrolled 898 women with unilateral breast cancer, mastectomy and an indication for chest wall and regional nodal radiation, with planned immediate or delayed reconstruction. It compared 42.56 Gy in 16 fractions over about 3 weeks (hypofractionated) with 50 Gy in 25 fractions over 5 weeks (conventional). Primary endpoint: reconstruction complications at 24 months (wound-healing problems, readmission, capsular contracture, unplanned surgery or loss of the reconstruction). Among the 825 evaluable patients, complications occurred in 12.3% with the short schedule (52/422) and 10.2% with the conventional one (41/403). The numerical difference of 2.1 percentage points, compatible with the study's uncertainty, fell within the prespecified noninferiority margin of 10 percentage points. Three-year locoregional recurrence was 1.5% vs 2.2%, not significantly different (median follow-up 3.9 years). Implant-only reconstruction had more complications than autologous reconstruction with either schedule.
Why it matters: this is a noninferiority result: it does not show superiority, nor does it rule out a risk difference smaller than the 10-point margin. Capsular contracture was assessed within the 24-month endpoint, but follow-up remains limited for later events and for long-term oncologic control. Cutting treatment from 5 to 3 weeks reduces travel and the load on radiotherapy units, which matters where access is limited, as in the Dominican Republic.

2. Proposed new breast implant patient decision checklist, open for public comment

[FDA regulatory: proposal under consultation] [United States]
On September 29, 2026, the Breast Surgery Collaborative Community (BSCC), a collaborative community of societies and other stakeholders, submitted to the FDA public docket a revised version of the "Breast Implant Patient Decision Checklist", the document reviewed with patients before they receive breast implants, which complements, and does not replace, informed consent. The proposal aims for a shorter, clearer checklist that supports shared decision-making. Comments are accepted until December 1, 2026 (docket FDA-2019-D-4467). This is not an FDA-approved update: the current checklist remains in force.
Why it matters: if the FDA adopts it, the document that accompanies breast implant decisions in the United States will change. For now nothing changes in practice; it is worth reading the proposed text and, if appropriate, commenting before the deadline.
