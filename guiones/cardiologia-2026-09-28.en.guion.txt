Good morning, colleagues. This cardiology bulletin covers August twenty-eighth to September twenty-eighth, twenty twenty-six. The month was dominated by the European Society of Cardiology Congress in Munich. We selected sixteen items verified at the source. In several cases, figures come from official society press releases and specialist coverage.

We begin with the new European heart failure guidelines, published on August twenty-eighth. The mildly reduced ejection fraction category is gone: only reduced, below fifty percent, and preserved remain. Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists receive Class one across all ejection fractions. Semaglutide and tirzepatide obtain Class two a in preserved ejection fraction with obesity. Critical reading: this reorganizes existing evidence rather than adding new data, and in our setting, drug access remains the real barrier.

The Australian Staree trial tested atorvastatin forty milligrams in nine thousand nine hundred seventy-one adults aged seventy or older without cardiovascular disease. Major cardiovascular events were six point zero versus eight point three percent. The hazard ratio was zero point seven zero. Disability-free survival did not change. Critical reading: the neutral disability result calls for shared decision-making rather than automatic prescribing.

The Japanese Premium trial omitted aspirin at primary angioplasty for S T segment elevation myocardial infarction. In two thousand two hundred eighty patients, death, infarction or stroke occurred in eleven point zero versus eight point five percent. The hazard ratio was one point three four, failing noninferiority. Critical reading: aspirin should not be withdrawn at primary angioplasty, though the low Japanese prasugrel dose limits extrapolation.

In the German Trick I H F trial, three hundred sixty patients with severe tricuspid regurgitation received transcatheter edge-to-edge repair or medical therapy alone. One-year survival free of death or hospitalization was seventy-three point nine versus forty-eight point four percent. Critical reading: it was open label, with the principal investigator's conflict of interest with the manufacturer.

In the Librexia trial, fourteen thousand one hundred ninety-four patients with acute coronary syndrome received milvexian or placebo. The primary outcome occurred in five point four versus five point one percent, and the trial stopped for futility. Critical reading: another setback for factor eleven inhibition in arterial disease.

In the Korean A Close trial, three thousand two hundred three high-risk patients received clopidogrel alone or dual antiplatelet therapy to twenty-four months. The net outcome was noninferior. But the ischemic composite was three point seven versus one point six percent. Critical reading: the net result masks a signal of excess ischemic events and death with de-escalation.

A sham-controlled trial compared atrial fibrillation ablation with sedation and vascular access alone in two hundred sixty-two patients. Quality of life at six months showed no significant difference. Freedom from atrial fibrillation was seventy-three versus fifty-two percent. Critical reading: the rhythm effect is confirmed, but the symptomatic benefit cannot be distinguished from a placebo effect.

In Korea, an open-label trial anticoagulated one thousand eight hundred three patients with atrial fibrillation and a single risk factor. The composite was zero point five versus one point five percent, with a hazard ratio of zero point three one. Critical reading: events were very few and only Korean patients were enrolled, so it confirms rather than changes practice.

The Poet two trial enrolled five hundred eight patients with left-sided endocarditis. Stopping antibiotics at clinical stabilization, at a median of twenty-six days, was noninferior to the standard course. Critical reading: relapses were more frequent with the short regimen, five versus one point six percent.

In nine hundred eighty-six patients with severe aortic stenosis and coronary disease, treating the valve first was noninferior to angioplasty first. Critical reading: it supports reassessing the real need for angioplasty afterward, with less bleeding.

The Acacia trial of aficamten was the first positive drug trial in non-obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, with five hundred seventeen patients. Critical reading: the quality-of-life difference was modest, about three points, and the sponsor is the manufacturer.

In India, the Time trial of one thousand five hundred seven patients tested nurse-coordinated collaborative care with a mobile app. Mortality was twenty-two percent lower. Critical reading: a low-cost model in a setting comparable to ours, though unblinded.

The Cardio Transform trial of eplontersen in transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy missed its primary endpoint. Critical reading: it suggests that adding a ribonucleic acid silencer on top of a stabilizer provides no demonstrable benefit.

Finally, the United States Food and Drug Administration authorized the first artificial intelligence electrocardiogram model for detecting S T segment elevation myocardial infarction. Critical reading: the evidence comes from registries, but the tool is of obvious interest for infarction networks such as many in the Dominican Republic.
