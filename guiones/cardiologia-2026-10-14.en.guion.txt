Good morning, colleagues. This is the cardiology news brief for October fourteenth, two thousand twenty-six. We cover October second to fourteenth, a fortnight shaped by three meetings: the Heart Failure Society of America in Phoenix, the European cardiothoracic surgeons in Barcelona, and the World Congress of Cardiology in Rio de Janeiro. Almost everything you will hear is a meeting presentation, not yet published in a peer-reviewed journal.

We begin with blood pressure. A meta-analysis in Jama Cardiology pooled twenty-seven randomized trials with nearly eighty-nine thousand participants. For every ten millimeters of mercury of systolic reduction, major cardiovascular events fell eleven percent in patients with chronic kidney disease, and sixteen percent in those without. But sustained declines in kidney filtration were about forty percent more frequent. Critical reading: the benefit appears real in the diseased kidney too, but there is little data below a filtration rate of thirty, and creatinine and potassium must be monitored.

The American College of Cardiology published its decision pathway for resistant hypertension: rule out pseudoresistance, measure pressure outside the office, and escalate treatment before considering renal denervation. Critical reading: it is expert consensus without new evidence, but useful in any clinic.

In non-obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy, Cytokinetics presented new analyses of the Acacia trial, aficamten versus placebo for thirty-six weeks. Fifty-three percent of treated patients responded in three or more clinical domains, versus thirteen percent on placebo. But ejection fraction fell below fifty percent in ten and a half percent of patients on aficamten, versus less than one percent on placebo. Critical reading: this is a manufacturer release, and that drop demands serial echocardiography during titration.

With tovinontrine, a phosphodiesterase nine inhibitor, the phase two Cycle one trial in heart failure with reduced ejection fraction found no significant natriuretic peptide reduction at any dose. Nothing changes in practice.

In obesity with diabetes, the Synchronize two trial, presented on October first and published in the New England Journal of Medicine, tested weekly survodutide in seven hundred fifty-five adults over seventy-six weeks. Weight loss reached up to thirteen percent, versus three percent with placebo. Critical reading: these are efficacy estimand figures, eighteen percent stopped because of gastrointestinal effects, and cardiovascular outcomes are still unknown.

The Heart Failure Society of America released its annual statistics report. Nearly one in four Americans will develop heart failure, and only eighteen percent of patients with reduced ejection fraction receive quadruple therapy. Critical reading: the gap between what we know and what we prescribe is the message for every health system, including ours.

From Barcelona, a subanalysis of the Vesalius trial evaluated evolocumab in one thousand four hundred thirty-two patients with prior coronary bypass surgery and no heart attack. After a median of four and a half years, the composite of events occurred in eighteen percent on evolocumab, versus twenty-five percent on placebo, and heart attacks were cut by more than half. Critical reading: this is a subgroup of an Amgen trial, and the hard endpoints did not reach significance; still, the bypass patient without a heart attack is at very high risk.

Also in Barcelona, a Dutch registry of more than three thousand surgical aortic valve patients was compared with the Dedicate trial. Surgery inside the trial had nearly three times the stroke rate of registry surgery, and the absolute mortality advantage of transcatheter valve implantation shrank from three point eight to two point one points. Critical reading: this is a complex statistical exercise, and Dedicate's five-year efficacy is still pending.

In arrhythmias, the Beat A F trial, from a single center in China, randomized five hundred two cardiac surgery patients to epicardial patches with amiodarone or saline. Postoperative atrial fibrillation occurred in thirteen and fifteen percent, with no significant difference. Critical reading: the trial was underpowered; nothing changes in prophylaxis.

In heart transplantation, Spanish transplants from controlled donation after circulatory death had equivalent one-year survival and now account for a third of activity. Critical reading: these are observational data, but a model our region should study.

From Rio de Janeiro, the Brazilian Society of Cardiology published its new heart failure guideline. It drops the mildly reduced ejection fraction category, treats any fraction below fifty percent as reduced, and includes a section on Chagas cardiomyopathy. Critical reading: it is the guideline closest to our reality, though it departs from the European and American guidelines.

On the agenda: T C T in San Diego, October thirty-first to November third, and the American Heart Association Scientific Sessions in Chicago, in November.

Thank you for listening. Until next month.
