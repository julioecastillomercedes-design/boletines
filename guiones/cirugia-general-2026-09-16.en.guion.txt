Good morning, colleagues. This is the general surgery bulletin for the week of September ninth to sixteenth, two thousand twenty-six. It was a moderate-volume week, with eight verified items.

We start with Eagle two, an international prospective cohort in three hundred thirty-two hospitals across sixty countries. It included two thousand eight hundred seventy-five right hemicolectomies or ileocecal resections with a single anastomosis. Thirty-day leak or collection was nine point one percent when the surgeon had completed the five free modules of the program. Without training, it was sixteen point four percent. The adjusted odds ratio was zero point five six. Mortality was two point nine versus five point zero percent. Critical reading: it is not randomized, and the untrained group had more emergencies, twenty-four versus fifteen percent. Still, the magnitude and near-zero cost make it reasonable to ask the whole colorectal team, including residents, to take the course.

On the abdominal wall, a Spanish randomized, prospective, double-blind trial was published online on September ninth in the surgery journal of the American Medical Association. It evaluates whether prophylactic mesh at wall closure after hepatic, biliary and pancreatic surgery reduces incisional hernia. Critical reading: this is the heaviest item of the week, but the abstract with the sample size and primary outcome figures could not be opened. Read it at the source before extrapolating data from the Prima trial to subcostal laparotomy.

An American hernia quality registry analyzed thirty-two thousand nine hundred fifty-six elective clean-wound ventral hernia repairs. In women versus men, surgical site infection was two point four versus one point six percent. The adjusted odds ratio was one point three one. Readmission was three point two versus two point five percent, a difference that was not significant after adjustment. Pragmatic two-year recurrence was nineteen point two versus fifteen point five percent. After multivariable adjustment, female sex was associated with worse hernia-specific quality of life and more pain. Critical reading: it is a voluntary, retrospective registry, but its size makes it necessary to include sex in consent and in the stratification of abdominal wall trials.

A Brazilian meta-analysis pooled four retrospective comparative studies, with seven hundred sixty-three patients, after abdominoperineal resection. Perineal hernia was more frequent with the minimally invasive route, with an odds ratio of four point one three and heterogeneity of zero percent. Bayesian analysis gave a ninety-nine point nine percent probability of higher risk with that route. Critical reading: these are only four retrospective series with heterogeneous follow-up. But the signal is consistent and supports planned perineal reconstruction, with mesh or flap, after laparoscopic or robotic resection.

In rectal cancer, an American national cohort included twenty-two thousand six hundred eighty-eight patients from eight hundred seventy-three centers. It assessed the Commission on Cancer standard requiring a synoptic report of the mesorectal excision. At hospitals meeting it, the positive circumferential margin rate was twelve versus fourteen percent. The adjusted odds ratio was zero point eight two. Incomplete mesorectal excision was nine versus fourteen percent. Critical reading: it is an association in an administrative database, with no long-term oncologic outcome. Still, it supports the idea that systematically auditing the specimen improves technique.

On circulating tumor deoxyribonucleic acid in colon cancer, the American College of Surgeons published a review of current data. In the Alliance trial, five-year disease-free survival was twenty-eight versus seventy-seven percent. In the Dynamic three trial, de-escalation in negative patients did not reach non-inferiority. Three-year recurrence-free survival was eighty-five versus eighty-eight percent. Critical reading: the test is prognostic but not predictive. The American National Comprehensive Cancer Network does not recommend using it to decide adjuvant therapy or surveillance outside trials.

In pancreatic surgery, a Spanish retrospective cohort used reinforcing mesh, adhesive and a ductal stent in duct-to-mucosa pancreaticojejunostomy. A meta-analysis of ten studies and one thousand thirteen patients was added. The pooled clinically relevant fistula rate was six point five percent. Versus the conventional technique, the odds ratio was zero point three five. Critical reading: the authors themselves conclude that the evidence is insufficient and heterogeneous. It is a hypothesis for a trial, not a reason to change technique.

Finally, according to Medtronic's September sixteenth announcement, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration cleared the LigaSure Maryland instrument for the Hugo robot. It seals and divides vessels and lymphatics up to seven millimeters in about two seconds. In the United States, Hugo remains cleared only for urology, and applications for general surgery are still pending. Critical reading: it is a manufacturer release with no new clinical data. It matters only as a signal that the platform is preparing for general surgery in the United States.

Always verify the primary source before applying any data. Thank you for listening. Until next month.
