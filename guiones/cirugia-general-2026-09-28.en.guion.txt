Good morning, colleagues. This is the general surgery bulletin for the period from August twenty-ninth to September twenty-eighth, twenty twenty-six. It was a thin month for randomized trials. There are seven verified items: we prefer to publish fewer rather than pad.

On the abdominal wall, a single-center, double-blind randomized trial in Seville enrolled one hundred forty-four high-risk patients. They underwent hepatobiliopancreatic surgery through a right subcostal laparotomy. Conventional closure was compared with closure reinforced by a retromuscular synthetic mesh. Radiological incisional hernia at twelve months was five point two percent with mesh versus twenty-one percent without. At twenty-four months it was eight point one percent versus thirty-seven point five percent. It was published in Jama Surgery on September ninth. The critical reading: the effect is large, but it is a single center, the abstract gives no risk ratios or intervals, and follow-up is only two years.

In colorectal surgery, the Eagle Two study appeared in the British Journal of Surgery on September eleventh. It is an international prospective cohort of two thousand eight hundred seventy-five patients undergoing right colectomy with a single anastomosis. In seventy-eight percent of cases, the surgeon had completed free online safe-anastomosis modules. In those cases, thirty-day leak or intra-abdominal collection was nine point one percent versus sixteen point four percent. The adjusted odds ratio was zero point five six, with a ninety-five percent confidence interval from zero point four one to zero point seven eight. The critical reading: it is observational, and surgeons who complete the course probably differ from those who do not. Still, the intervention is free and scalable.

In gastric cancer, the I Green Go study assessed indocyanine green during second-tier lymphadenectomy. It is a prospective observational study in fifteen Italian centers. It included three hundred sixteen patients operated on by minimally invasive surgery. Residual fluorescence prompted additional dissection in sixty-one patients, nineteen point three percent. Stage migration occurred in only three of three hundred sixteen patients, zero point nine percent. The critical reading: fluorescence adds nodes but almost never changes the stage. It does not support routine use outside research protocols, and there is no randomized comparator.

In pancreatic surgery, Jama Surgery published on September second a machine-learning model based on circulating exosomal microRNA. It aims to predict occult liver metastasis before surgery for pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. It is a retrospective case-control study of three hundred seventy-two patients. The seven-microRNA model reached an area under the curve of zero point eight nine nine in training. In the two external cohorts it reached zero point eight seven six and zero point eight six two. The critical reading: the design is retrospective and the authors call for prospective validation. Until then it is translational research, not a tool to decide whom not to operate on.

On localized appendiceal adenocarcinoma, Jama Surgery published on September ninth a retrospective cohort from the United States. It included four hundred thirty-nine patients with stage one to three disease, with external validation in one hundred twenty-eight stage two patients. Relapse was six percent in stage two and nineteen point five percent in stage three. Adjuvant chemotherapy was not associated with better relapse-free survival, with a hazard ratio of zero point nine eight. The critical reading: it is retrospective with possible indication bias, but it supports individualizing adjuvant therapy by histology and stage.

At the Clinical Congress of the American College of Surgeons, an abstract on acute appendicitis was presented. After matching, it compared fifteen thousand six hundred sixteen patients treated with appendectomy and fifteen thousand two hundred ninety-five treated with antibiotics alone. Subsequent colorectal cancer was diagnosed in zero point seven versus one point seven percent. That is a fifty-eight percent relative reduction. The critical reading: it is retrospective, not peer reviewed and carries an obvious bias, because appendectomy removes occult tumors. It does not demonstrate a protective effect, but it underlines follow-up colonoscopy in adults treated without surgery.

On the regulatory side, Medtronic announced on September sixteenth that the United States Food and Drug Administration cleared the LigaSure Maryland vessel sealer for the Hugo robot. Hugo is cleared in the United States only for urology, and the general surgery submission remains pending. The critical reading: there are no comparative clinical data, and for Latin America what will matter is the cost per procedure and instrument availability, not the press release.

In trauma, emergency surgery, bariatrics and general surgical oncology, no verifiable items were found within the period.
