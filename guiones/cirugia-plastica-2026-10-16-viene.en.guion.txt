Good morning, colleagues. This edition of what's next in plastic surgery looks at the coming six months, from now through mid-April two thousand twenty-seven. We include only dates confirmed in an official source. This selection includes three: one regulatory deadline and two meetings. This edition's search identified no regulatory decisions, trial readouts or guidelines with a confirmed date in this window. If any appear, we will add them.

First, on December first, two thousand twenty-six, public comment closes on a proposed new patient decision checklist for breast implants. A breast surgery collaborative community submitted it to the Food and Drug Administration on September twenty-ninth. It is the document reviewed with patients before surgery, and it complements informed consent rather than replacing it. According to its authors, the proposal aims to make it shorter, clearer and more practical in clinic. Critical take: this is a proposal, not an approved decision. After the deadline there is no known date for a final version, and the final text may change or not be adopted. In the meantime, the current checklist remains in force. Anyone who wants to weigh in can do so in the public docket before that date.

Second, from January twenty-eighth to thirtieth, two thousand twenty-seven, the IMCAS world congress takes place in Paris and online. It brings plastic surgery, dermatology and aging science together in one forum. We did not verify its detailed program, so we do not anticipate its content.

Third, from April fifteenth to eighteenth, two thousand twenty-seven, The Aesthetic Society holds its annual meeting in Miami Beach, called The Aesthetic MEET this year. It is the society's annual congress, with sessions on surgical and nonsurgical techniques. The full program and faculty are not yet published.

A note on reading what comes out of these meetings. An abstract presented at a meeting does not replace the full article and does not allow all of its methods to be assessed. Four things are worth checking. First, whether there is a comparison group, and whether allocation was random. Second, how long follow-up lasted, because many complications appear months or years later. Third, whether the outcome matters to patients or is an intermediate measure. And fourth, who funded the study and what conflicts of interest the authors declared. When a result is published in full, we will review it against that same bar.

In short: a deadline to comment on the checklist that accompanies breast implant decisions, and two meetings we will follow closely. We will come back with results once peer-reviewed publications are available.

Thank you for listening.
