Good morning, colleagues. This is the first plastic surgery bulletin from MedBulletins. It covers September sixteenth to October sixteenth, two thousand twenty-six. This selection includes three items, all with a confirmed date and source.

The main story is the R-T Charm trial. Its results were presented at a radiation oncology meeting in two thousand twenty-four, and the full paper was published on October twelfth in the Journal of Clinical Oncology. It is a phase three randomized trial from the United States and Canada, with eight hundred ninety-eight women with breast cancer, mastectomy and planned reconstruction, who needed radiation to the chest wall and regional nodes. It compared a short schedule, forty-two point five six gray in sixteen sessions over about three weeks, with the conventional fifty gray in twenty-five sessions over five weeks. The primary endpoint was reconstruction complications at two years. They occurred in twelve point three percent with the short schedule and ten point two percent with the conventional one. The numerical difference, two point one points, is compatible with the study's uncertainty and fell within the ten percentage point noninferiority margin set in advance. Three-year local and regional recurrence was one point five versus two point two percent, not significantly different. Critical take: this is a noninferiority result, not superiority, and it does not rule out a risk difference smaller than that margin. Follow-up is still limited for later events. Even so, cutting two weeks of treatment matters where access to radiotherapy is limited.

Second item. On September twenty-ninth, a breast surgery collaborative community submitted to the Food and Drug Administration a proposed new patient decision checklist for breast implants. It is the document reviewed with patients before surgery, and it complements informed consent rather than replacing it. The proposal aims for a shorter, clearer list. Note that this is a proposal open for public comment until December first. It is not an approved update, and the current checklist remains in force.

Third item. The annual meeting of the American Society of Plastic Surgeons runs in Houston from October fifteenth to eighteenth. It started the day before this bulletin closed, so we do not yet report its data. In the next issue we will review what is published with verifiable data.

On the near-term agenda, the world congress of the International Society of Aesthetic Plastic Surgery takes place in Cancún from October twenty-eighth to thirty-first, with a residents' symposium on the twenty-seventh.
