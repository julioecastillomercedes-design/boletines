Good morning, colleagues. This is the monthly dermatology bulletin from Boletines Médicos, covering September sixteenth to October sixteenth, twenty twenty-six. The month centered on the European Academy of Dermatology and Venereology congress in Vienna. Much of the data comes from industry and is not yet published, so please listen with caution.

We start with psoriasis. Two oral tyrosine kinase two inhibitors went head to head in a randomized, double-blind trial of six hundred six adults. Zasocitinib, from the Japanese company Takeda, beat deucravacitinib. At sixteen weeks, complete skin clearance was thirty-six point five percent versus thirteen point nine percent. The caveat: the study was short, had no biologic arm, and is not yet fully published.

Also in psoriasis, icotrokinra, an oral peptide that blocks the interleukin twenty-three receptor, held its response at difficult sites through two years. In the treated arm at week one hundred twelve, seventy percent had clear or almost clear skin, and eighty-nine percent had complete genital clearance. There was no control group after week sixteen, and several authors work for the manufacturer.

In atopic dermatitis, nemolizumab showed durability. In the Arcadia extension, with one thousand nine hundred three patients, response held for up to three years. Up to ninety-one percent kept a seventy-five percent improvement in eczema severity. Keep in mind these are observed cases, which tend to inflate the numbers.

From China comes telikibart, a new antibody against the interleukin four receptor alpha, the same target as dupilumab, though with no head-to-head comparison. In four hundred fifty adults, sixty-nine point six percent reached a seventy-five percent improvement at sixteen weeks, versus twenty-two point five percent on placebo. The population was only Chinese, and conjunctivitis rates were not reported.

Pfizer presented tilrekimig, a trispecific antibody that blocks interleukin four, interleukin thirteen and thymic stromal lymphopoietin at once. In a phase two study of one hundred eighty-eight adults, response ranged from forty-eight to sixty-three percent, versus at most twenty percent on placebo. Dosing every four weeks is appealing, but the study was small and lasted only sixteen weeks.

In hidradenitis suppurativa, izokibep, an interleukin seventeen A inhibitor, met its primary endpoint in a phase three trial of two hundred fifty-eight patients. On a stringent measure, a ninety percent reduction in inflammatory burden, twenty-three point six percent responded, versus eight point one percent on placebo. Encouraging, though the company did not release the primary endpoint figure.

On September twenty-fifth, the Food and Drug Administration approved baricitinib for severe alopecia areata from age twelve. On four milligrams daily, forty-two percent of adolescents regrew at least eighty percent of scalp hair, versus five percent on placebo. Long-term safety of Janus kinase inhibitors in minors still needs weighing.

In vitiligo, among patients who stayed on oral upadacitinib, facial repigmentation of at least seventy-five percent rose to fifty-five point one and forty-seven point four percent at seventy-six weeks in the two trials. These are observed cases with no control group. With ritlecitinib, in the Tranquillo two trial of one thousand five hundred sixty-seven adults, the figure was twenty-one point nine percent at one year, versus two point four percent on placebo. Both programs are manufacturer data, and full publications are pending.

In skin cancer, a slightly older item. On September fourteenth, the agency approved aminolevulinic acid gel with photodynamic therapy for superficial basal cell carcinoma. Complete clinical and histological response was sixty-six percent, versus five percent with vehicle. Surgery remains the standard, and there is no direct comparison with it.

In prevention, the Flare trial enrolled three hundred sixty-four families of melanoma survivors. A family telehealth program improved sun protection, but did not reduce sunburns over follow-up. A negative result, but an honest and useful one.

Finally, a safety alert. Six of eighteen over-the-counter skin products sold online contained undeclared clobetasol, and the strongest was more than nine times the approved strength. In perioral dermatitis, atrophy or atypical tinea, ask about unregistered creams.

There was no news this period in infectious dermatology, dermoscopy or artificial intelligence.

On the calendar: the American Dermatological Association meets October twenty-first to twenty-fifth in Philadelphia. The American Society for Dermatologic Surgery meets November fifth to eighth in San Diego. And the dermatopathology meeting runs November nineteenth to twenty-second in Atlanta.

Thank you for listening. Until next month.
