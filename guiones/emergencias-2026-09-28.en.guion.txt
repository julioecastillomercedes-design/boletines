Good morning, colleagues. This bulletin covers August twenty-ninth to September twenty-eighth, twenty twenty-six. It was a thin month for large trials in the specialty. There are seven items, each verified against its source, and no recycled material was added as filler.

We start with the Precise trial, presented at the European Society of Cardiology Congress and published in The Lancet. It compared the zero and one hour troponin pathway with the zero and three hour pathway. It included sixty-seven thousand six hundred twenty-four presentations with suspected myocardial infarction in twenty hospitals. Death or new infarction at thirty days was one point one percent versus one point two percent. Median emergency department stay was three hundred nine minutes in both groups. Critical reading: it validates the safety of the algorithm, but the bottleneck is flow, not the laboratory. Stepped-wedge designs are sensitive to temporal trends. According to the European Society of Cardiology press release, funding was Swiss public and industry, including Idorsia and Roche Diagnostics.

In the same session, an individual patient data meta-analysis of five trials was presented, with one hundred ten thousand nine hundred thirty-three presentations. The zero and one hour pathway had similar safety, with no reduction in length of stay and no increase in direct discharge. Critical reading: there is no peer-reviewed publication yet, so the figures are preliminary.

The Target trial, published in the New England Journal of Medicine, enrolled three thousand one hundred seventy patients in the United Kingdom. They had chest pain, infarction ruled out and intermediate high-sensitivity troponin. They were randomized to coronary computed tomography angiography or standard care, at a median of six days after presentation. At three years, infarction or cardiac death occurred in seven point one versus seven point three percent. The adjusted hazard ratio was zero point nine five. Critical reading: an intermediate troponin alone should not mandate coronary imaging. The scan was not performed in the emergency department, and the senior author reports consulting for Roche Diagnostics.

The American College of Emergency Physicians published new consensus guidelines on unscheduled procedural sedation. Decisions should focus on sedation depth and ventilation rather than the specific drug. Urgent procedures should not be delayed solely because of fasting, and the Mallampati score should not be required. Capnography, oximetry with audible tone, cardiac rhythm and blood pressure should be used routinely whenever feasible. Ketofol is as safe and effective as propofol. Critical reading: this is a Delphi consensus, not a trial. In Dominican departments with limited capnography, monitoring is the first gap to close.

A randomized trial on pediatric asthma exacerbations was announced on August twenty-sixth, outside the period. Early bilevel positive airway pressure with continuous albuterol did not shorten continuous albuterol therapy compared with a sham device. The mean was eight point three versus eight point seven hours. According to Physician's Weekly coverage, it enrolled sixty-six children and was stopped for futility. Critical reading: there is no basis for routine use in exacerbations that respond to conventional treatment.

In the Dominican Republic, on August thirty-first, the Ministry of Public Health launched a national assessment of its emergency and critical care system. It uses a standardized World Health Organization tool and evaluates five domains. Next come a survey, a national consensus workshop and a report with a road map. Critical reading: it provides no figures yet, but it sets the public-policy agenda for the coming years. The voice of emergency physicians in the survey and the workshop will decide whether the road map reflects reality.

An American College of Emergency Physicians poll of ten thousand adults was released on September fourteenth. Eighty-one percent want a physician on site in the emergency department. Fifty-two percent would delay or avoid care if at risk of boarding. Critical reading: it is an opinion poll with an advocacy interest, not a clinical study, but it quantifies the deterrent effect of crowding.

Looking ahead, the American College of Emergency Physicians Scientific Assembly takes place in Chicago, October fifth to eighth. The Japanese Association for Acute Medicine congress meets in Nagasaki, October twenty-seventh to twenty-ninth.

Thank you for listening. Until next month.
