Good morning, colleagues. This is what's next in endocrinology, dated October twentieth, twenty twenty-six. We review four pending regulatory decisions between now and the end of December: two in the United States and two in the European Union. Only one has a published target date; the others are estimates, and we present them as such.

First, relacorilant in Cushing's syndrome. It is an oral selective glucocorticoid receptor modulator with no antiprogestogenic effect. The US Food and Drug Administration turned it down at the end of December twenty twenty-five: it acknowledged that the main trial, called GRACE, met its goal, but said it could not reach a favorable balance of benefit and risk. Corcept resubmitted in June with additional analyses, and the new decision date is December seventeenth, twenty twenty-six. GRACE enrolled one hundred fifty-two patients with hypertension, hyperglycemia or both; in the randomized withdrawal phase, those who stayed on the drug were eighty-three percent less likely to lose blood pressure control than those on placebo. Today, when surgery does not cure, we use osilodrostat, metyrapone, ketoconazole, pasireotide or mifepristone, and the latter causes hypokalemia and endometrial thickening. The critical take: the endpoint is blood pressure, a surrogate, in a manufacturer-funded trial, and the agency has already rejected it once on the same data.

Second, CagriSema, the weekly combination of cagrilintide, an amylin analogue, with semaglutide, for obesity. It was submitted in the United States in December twenty twenty-five, and Novo Nordisk places the decision in the fourth quarter of twenty twenty-six, with no official date published. In the REDEFINE one trial, with about three thousand four hundred adults without diabetes, weight loss at sixty-eight weeks was about twenty percent, versus three percent with placebo; in people with type two diabetes it was almost fourteen percent versus just over three. The critical take: in the REDEFINE four trial, against maximum-dose tirzepatide, it failed to show non-inferiority, at about twenty percent versus almost twenty-four, and there are no cardiovascular outcome data yet.

Third, oral orforglipron, brand name Foundayo, in Europe. On October sixteenth, the European Medicines Agency's medicines committee recommended approval for weight management and for type two diabetes. The European Commission usually decides within about two months, so authorization is expected around December. It is a non-peptide agonist of the glucagon-like peptide one receptor taken once daily, with or without food. According to the agency, weight loss in the obesity trials was five to eleven percent depending on dose, versus about two percent with placebo, and in diabetes, glycated hemoglobin fell by one point two to one point nine points, more than with dapagliflozin. The critical take: it produces less weight loss than the injectables, no cardiovascular trial has been published yet, and access will depend on price.

Fourth, weekly navepegritide in achondroplasia, for pediatric endocrinology. It received a positive European committee opinion in October for children from age two with open growth plates, and Ascendis expects the Commission's decision in the fourth quarter. In the ApproaCH trial, with eighty-four children, growth velocity was almost six centimeters a year, versus four and a half with placebo. Today the alternative in Europe, vosoritide, is injected daily. The critical take: growth velocity is a surrogate, and there are no data yet on final height or complications.
