Good morning, colleagues. This issue covers August twenty-eighth to September twenty-eighth, two thousand twenty-six. It was a month with more regulatory and industry signals than published phase three trials.

The United States Food and Drug Administration approved daraxonrasib, a Ras-family inhibitor, for metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma after at least one systemic line. In the Rasolute three-oh-two trial of five hundred patients, median overall survival was thirteen point two versus six point seven months. The hazard ratio was zero point four zero. It is the biggest second-line advance in decades, but the trial was open-label, and price and availability in Latin America are unknown.

The Baveno eight consensus on portal hypertension was published. It rules out clinically significant portal hypertension with liver stiffness of fifteen kilopascals or less. Platelets must be one hundred fifty times ten to the ninth per liter or more. It recommends carvedilol to prevent decompensation when the probability is seventy-five percent or more. It also defines recompensation. The critical reading: many new statements rest on low-level evidence and expert opinion.

The human medicines committee of the European Medicines Agency recommended glepaglutide for short bowel syndrome. In the Ease one trial of one hundred six patients, weekly parenteral volume fell by five point one three liters, versus two point eight five with placebo. According to Zealand, it would be the first major advance in Europe in over a decade. The European Commission decision is expected in about two months.

In primary biliary cholangitis, the Elative trial reported two-year data. At week one hundred four, biochemical response with elafibranor was sixty-four point three percent, eighteen of twenty-eight. No placebo patient reached it. The critical reading: very few patients remain at the late cutoff, and hard clinical outcomes are missing.

A Danish cohort of three hundred fifty-four thousand six hundred twenty-four people estimated ten-year cirrhosis risk. It was zero point one four percent with one to seven drinks per week and four point six percent above forty-two. The population attributable fraction for alcohol was fifty-nine point nine percent. It is observational, and intake was self-reported.

In chronic portal vein thrombosis without cirrhosis, spleen stiffness of forty kilopascals or less gave a negative predictive value of ninety-seven percent in derivation. It would avoid forty-one to forty-three percent of endoscopies. It is retrospective and needs prospective validation before endoscopies are skipped.

BioNTech terminated its phase two trial of the messenger ribonucleic acid vaccine autogene cevumeran in resected colorectal cancer. The safety committee found a numerical imbalance in overall survival versus surveillance. No figures have been released. It is a serious setback for these vaccines as monotherapy, and the full publication is needed.

Akeso announced that its phase three Harmoni trial met its overall survival endpoint in advanced biliary tract cancer. It compared ivonescimab plus chemotherapy against durvalumab plus chemotherapy. Data will be presented on October twenty-fifth in Madrid. Until we see the hazard ratio, sample size and population, it cannot be judged.

A British analysis of about nine hundred eighty thousand adults linked very hot drinks to triple the risk of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma. Hot drinks carried almost double the risk. Risk depended on temperature, not on tea or coffee. Temperature was self-reported, and no hazard ratios or intervals were released.

Teva reported a phase two a trial of fifty celiac patients with an anti-interleukin fifteen antibody. Under gluten challenge, the villus to crypt ratio fell by zero point four three, versus zero point eight eight with placebo. These are press-release data from a small sample. A gluten-free diet remains the treatment.

In ulcerative colitis, Spyre presented the open-label part of the Skyline trial of a long-acting anti-interleukin twenty-three. In forty-four patients, clinical remission at week twelve was twenty percent. Without placebo or blinding, these figures cannot be compared with the class.

Finally, the thirty-fourth Dominican Congress of Gastroenterology was held in Miches, September seventeenth to twentieth. We found no abstracts or data published from the congress. Its value this month is as an agenda and a professional network.

Thank you for listening. Until next month.
