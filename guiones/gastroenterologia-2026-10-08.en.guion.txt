Good morning, colleagues. This newscast covers September eighth to October eighth, two thousand twenty-six. The month was dominated by hepatology, a guideline on preventive care in cirrhosis, a European surveillance trial after adenomas, and a negative fecal transplantation trial. We include six items; we prefer fewer items to filler.

The European Epos Two trial, published in the New England Journal of Medicine, enrolled ten thousand seven hundred ninety-nine adults after complete removal of high-risk adenomas. It compared a first surveillance colonoscopy at five years with surveillance at three and five years. At the five point five year interim analysis, cumulative colorectal cancer incidence was zero point seven seven versus zero point eight two percent. Surveillance at five years was noninferior. Colorectal cancer deaths were three versus two. Critical reading: it is an interim analysis and depends on a high-quality index colonoscopy, which is not always the case in our setting. Guidelines have not yet changed.

In hepatitis delta, Mirum Pharmaceuticals reported phase three results from the Azure One trial of brelovitug, a subcutaneous monoclonal antibody. One hundred fifty-three treatment-naive patients were randomized. At week twenty-four, the combined response was fifty-six and forty-five percent in the active arms, versus zero percent in the delayed arm. Virologic response was eighty-six and eighty-five percent. There were no serious adverse events in the active arms. Critical reading: it is the first robust alternative to bulevirtide, but these are press-release data. The comparator was delayed treatment, with no placebo, at only twenty-four weeks and with no durability data.

The Norwegian Refit Two trial, published in The Lancet, is the largest trial of fecal transplantation in irritable bowel syndrome. Four hundred fifty adults received a single enema of donor stool or their own stool. Response at day ninety was forty percent with donor stool versus thirty-eight percent with placebo. The difference was not significant. Critical reading: fecal transplantation should not be offered for irritable bowel syndrome outside trials, including in the Dominican Republic. Still, it does not rule out benefit with repeated dosing or upper delivery.

The American College of Gastroenterology published a guideline on preventive care in cirrhosis with eighteen recommendations. It strongly recommends carvedilol or endoscopic band ligation as primary prophylaxis for high-risk varices. It also recommends ultrasound and alpha-fetoprotein every six months for liver cancer surveillance, vaccination, and smoking cessation. It recommends against aspirin to prevent liver cancer. Critical reading: nearly all evidence is low certainty. But it is an inexpensive checklist: carvedilol, vaccines and semiannual surveillance require no costly technology.

The European medicines committee adopted a positive opinion for glepaglutide, a long-acting analogue in a prefilled pen, for adults with short bowel syndrome. According to the agency's summary, it reduced weekly parenteral support volume more than placebo after twenty-four weeks of twice-weekly dosing. The European Commission decision is pending. Critical reading: the agency gives no effect sizes, and access in the Caribbean will be limited and costly.

After re-examination, the European agency upheld its negative opinion on Xervyteg, a pooled allogeneic fecal microbiota product for acute graft-versus-host disease. Critical reading: it sets a high regulatory bar. Together with Refit Two, the month sends a message of caution about microbiota therapy beyond Clostridioides difficile.

In inflammatory bowel disease, we could not verify any new phase three publications in the period. Abivax announced seven obefazimod abstracts for European Gastroenterology Week. We also found no verifiable high-impact publications in pancreas, biliary endoscopy, reflux, Helicobacter pylori or gastrointestinal bleeding.

Upcoming meetings: the American College of Gastroenterology annual meeting, October ninth to fourteenth in Nashville. European Gastroenterology Week, October seventeenth to twentieth in Barcelona. And the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases meeting, November fifth to ninth in Denver. Thank you for listening. Until next month.
