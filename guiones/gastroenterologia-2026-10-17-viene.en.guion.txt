Good morning, colleagues. This is what is coming in gastroenterology and hepatology over the next six months, from mid October twenty twenty-six to April twenty twenty-seven. There are four milestones with verifiable dates: a regulatory decision in hepatitis B, two congresses, and a pediatric decision in inflammatory bowel disease.

First, bepirovirsen. The United States Food and Drug Administration set October twenty-sixth, twenty twenty-six, as its decision date, under priority review. It is an antisense oligonucleotide aimed at functional cure of chronic hepatitis B, meaning sustained loss of surface antigen off treatment. In the B-Well one and two trials, randomized, double-blind and placebo-controlled, with more than eighteen hundred patients already on nucleoside analogues and surface antigen at or below three thousand international units per milliliter, functional cure was nineteen percent versus zero with placebo, and twenty-six percent when baseline antigen was at or below one thousand. Today, nucleoside analogues suppress the virus but rarely cure it, and treatment is usually indefinite. Critical take: eight in ten patients are not cured, the effect depends heavily on baseline antigen, the data are sponsor-funded, and access in our region will be slow.

Second, United European Gastroenterology Week, held in Barcelona from October seventeenth to twentieth. On October nineteenth, efficacy and safety data from the forty-four-week maintenance part of the phase three ABTECT trial of obefazimod, an oral agent that up-regulates micro R N A one twenty-four, will be presented in moderate to severe ulcerative colitis, followed on October twentieth by results in patients with and without prior inadequate response to advanced therapies. The company still plans to file for United States approval before the end of twenty twenty-six. Many patients lose response to biologics and small molecules, so an oral option with a different mechanism would be welcome. Critical take: what is public so far are sponsor press releases; wait for full figures, long-term safety and peer review.

Third, The Liver Meeting of the American Association for the Study of Liver Diseases, in Denver, from November fifth to ninth. The embargo on late-breaking abstracts lifts on November fifth. It is one of the venues where metabolic steatohepatitis, primary biliary cholangitis and viral hepatitis data usually appear. Critical take: late-breaking abstracts are often preliminary and not fully peer reviewed.

Fourth, intravenous vedolizumab in children aged two and older with moderate to severe ulcerative colitis or Crohn's disease. The Food and Drug Administration accepted the application, with a decision expected in the first quarter of twenty twenty-seven, according to the company, based on the KEPLER and WEBB trials with clinical remission at week fifty-four. Today it is widely used off label in pediatrics. Critical take: the Crohn's trial was still ongoing at filing, and there is no comparison with placebo or with another active drug.
