Good morning, colleagues. This obstetrics and gynecology bulletin covers August eighteenth to September eighteenth, twenty twenty-six. It includes ten verified items. It was a moderate-volume month, with no new drug approvals or new phase three trials in gynecologic oncology. The strongest material is in contraception, ovarian cancer prevention and guidelines.

We begin with ovarian cancer prevention. On August twenty-fifth, the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists reinforced opportunistic salpingectomy. It recommends routine bilateral salpingectomy at every hysterectomy, and in any gynecologic surgery entering the peritoneal cavity when fertility is not desired. Complete salpingectomy is the preferred procedure for permanent contraception. It cites a British Columbia cohort of forty thousand five hundred twenty-seven salpingectomies, with a serous cancer hazard ratio of zero point two two. Critical reading: the evidence remains observational, but it is consistent and the cost is minimal, including at postcesarean sterilization.

In obstetrics, the manufacturer announced on September ninth that the United States Food and Drug Administration granted breakthrough therapy designation to certolizumab pegol. The aim is to prevent adverse placental outcomes in pregnant women with antiphospholipid syndrome and a positive lupus anticoagulant. In the single-arm phase two Impact trial, nine of forty-five patients, twenty percent, had the composite outcome. Critical reading: there was no concurrent control, and the designation is not an approval. The standard of care remains aspirin plus heparin.

In contraception, a Swedish trial in the Journal of the American Medical Association tested intrauterine mepivacaine. It enrolled three hundred seventy nulliparous women receiving a reduced-diameter intrauterine device. On a one hundred millimeter visual scale, mean pain during placement was forty-three point eight with mepivacaine versus fifty-eight point six with saline. Critical reading: it is cheap and needle-free, but the reduction is moderate, about fifteen millimeters out of one hundred, and it does not replace other analgesic measures.

A randomized crossover trial at five Chinese hospitals evaluated real-time artificial intelligence during fetal ultrasound. According to Omnicuris coverage, it included one thousand five hundred eighty-four high-risk pregnancies. Sensitivity for ten intracranial malformations rose by eight point seven percentage points. Specificity met the five percent noninferiority margin. According to MedSci, the benefit was greater among less-experienced operators. Critical reading: the system is validated only in a Chinese population, but that benefit is exactly what a country short of fetal-medicine specialists would need.

The Society of Family Planning issued its first guideline on deep or nonpalpable contraceptive implants. If either end cannot be palpated with certainty, it calls for imaging before any removal attempt, with ultrasound first line. Deep placement occurred in eight point eight per one thousand insertions. Critical reading: this is a consensus guideline, not a trial; in our setting, the core message is do not fish blindly.

In Bangladesh, a national study of six thousand six hundred thirty-one pregnant women found gestational diabetes in nineteen point four percent. Diabetes in pregnancy was found in eight point one percent. Critical reading: the burden is much higher than previously estimated. For the Caribbean, with rising obesity, it argues for universal screening with the oral glucose tolerance test.

From Moscow, a retrospective study compared aortic balloon occlusion with a tourniquet in cesareans for placenta accreta spectrum. In thirty-two patients, median blood loss was seven hundred twenty-five versus two thousand two hundred twenty-five milliliters. The difference in hysterectomies was not significant. Critical reading: there were only eight patients in the intervention arm, with no randomization.

In oncology, a network meta-analysis of seven phase three trials and three thousand nine hundred thirty patients assessed first-line maintenance with poly adenosine diphosphate ribose polymerase inhibitors. Benefit was greatest with breast cancer susceptibility gene mutations, with a hazard ratio of zero point three zero for olaparib. No regimen outperformed another. Critical reading: it does not change practice; genetic and homologous recombination deficiency testing remains the key.

From Istanbul, a retrospective cohort of one hundred ninety-two benign hysterectomies compared vaginal natural orifice endoscopic surgery with laparoscopy. Rescue analgesia was twenty-three point nine versus sixty percent. Critical reading: the design is retrospective and single-center, but consistent with prior randomized evidence favoring the vaginal route.

Finally, a Polish systematic review of forty-four studies confirmed dienogest as first line in endometriosis. Critical reading: it brings no new data.
