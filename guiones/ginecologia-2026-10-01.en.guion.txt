Good morning, colleagues. This is the obstetrics and gynecology bulletin for August thirtieth to October first, two thousand twenty-six. It was a slow month: ten verified items and no major new randomized trial.

We start with glucagon-like peptide one receptor agonists in pregnancy. A systematic review with meta-analysis and international consensus, released by a Lancet journal, pooled seven studies with more than forty-three thousand exposed women. It found no significant association with preterm birth, fetal or neonatal death, cesarean delivery, preeclampsia or small-for-gestational-age newborns. Early pregnancy loss was thirty-one percent more common among exposed women. The consensus keeps the recommendation to stop these drugs before a planned pregnancy. Critical reading: all the evidence is observational, and the early loss figure mixes miscarriage with elective termination. The figures come from the press summary, without confidence intervals.

In platinum-resistant recurrent ovarian cancer, Japan approved pembrolizumab with paclitaxel, with or without bevacizumab, in September. The basis is Keynote Bee ninety-six, a double-blind trial with six hundred forty-three patients. Median progression-free survival was eight point three versus six point four months, with a hazard ratio of zero point seven zero. Median overall survival was seventeen point seven versus fourteen point zero months, with a hazard ratio of zero point eight two. Critical reading: the gain is modest, three point seven months, and the trial is the manufacturer's. No benefit was seen in the small Japanese subgroup.

In the same setting, the European Medicines Agency's human medicines committee recommended authorizing relacorilant with nab-paclitaxel. In the open-label Rosella trial, with three hundred eighty-one patients, median progression-free survival was six point five versus five point five months. Median overall survival was sixteen point zero versus eleven point nine months, with a hazard ratio of zero point six five. Critical reading: the trial was open-label and sponsored by the manufacturer, and the progression gain is one month. It is an opinion, not yet an authorization, and the drug is not registered in the Dominican Republic.

At the same meeting, the European committee recommended senaparib, a parp inhibitor, as maintenance in advanced ovarian cancer. In the Flames trial, median progression-free survival was not reached with senaparib versus thirteen point six months with placebo. The hazard ratio was zero point four three, in an interim analysis. Critical reading: there is no head-to-head comparison with olaparib or niraparib, overall survival is missing, and the trial was run only in China.

In pelvic floor surgery, ten-year follow-up of a German randomized trial of laparoscopic sacrocolpopexy was published. It compared absorbable with nonabsorbable sutures for vaginal mesh fixation. Of one hundred ninety-five women enrolled, one hundred four had ten-year data. One hundred two were at apical stage zero, with no difference between groups. Critical reading: it supports absorbable sutures without sacrificing apical durability. But only fifty-three percent of the cohort reached ten years, and there is no formal non-inferiority test.

In ovarian cancer with a rare mutation of the Tee Pee fifty-three gene, the manufacturer reported interim data for rezatapopt. Among seventy-six evaluable patients, the objective response rate was forty-six percent, with a median duration of ten point zero months. Critical reading: this is a manufacturer press release, not peer reviewed, with no comparator and with investigator-assessed response. Today it changes nothing in practice.

In the region, Costa Rica presented its national roadmap to eliminate cervical cancer between two thousand twenty-six and two thousand thirty. The country diagnoses an average of two hundred seventy new cases a year and records more than one hundred deaths annually. It adopts the ninety, seventy, ninety targets for two thousand thirty. It is a policy announcement, with no results yet. It is useful as a roadmap model for the Caribbean, including the Dominican Republic.

According to an Efe wire story, Roche Diagnostics and the Pan American Health Organization signed an agreement. It seeks to accelerate access to human papillomavirus screening tests. The region has fifteen point one cases per one hundred thousand women, almost four times the elimination threshold. Critical reading: the story was produced with Roche's support and reports no prices, volumes or countries.

A retrospective observational study in the journal Menopause analyzed two hundred sixty women who started hormone therapy. According to the society's press release, severe mood symptoms fell from sixty-two point three to twenty-four point six percent. Critical reading: there is no control group, and hormone therapy is not indicated as a treatment for depression.

Finally, on September third the U.S. Food and Drug Administration reported supply difficulties with estradiol patches due to rising demand. This is not clinical data but a market signal; it is wise to plan for other estradiol formulations.

Always verify the primary source before applying any data. Thank you for listening. Until next month.
