This edition covers what is coming in obstetrics and gynecology between late October twenty twenty-six and March twenty twenty-seven: phase three data at the European oncology congress, two pending regulatory decisions, and the two big spring meetings. For each one we say how firm the date is.

First, the European Society for Medical Oncology congress, in Madrid, from October twenty-third to twenty-seventh. Two endometrial cancer trials are in the presidential symposium. The first tests sacituzumab tirumotecan, an antibody drug conjugate aimed at the trop two protein, against physician's choice of doxorubicin or paclitaxel in seven hundred seventy-six patients with advanced or recurrent disease after platinum and immunotherapy. The company reported in May that it improved overall and progression-free survival in certain patients, at an interim analysis. Today, after platinum and immunotherapy, we are left with chemotherapies that give short responses. The second trial compares pembrolizumab alone with carboplatin and paclitaxel as first-line treatment for mismatch repair deficient disease, a key question: can these patients skip chemotherapy. Critical take: both are company trials, and we still need full toxicity and survival figures to judge the real benefit.

Second, the European Commission is due to decide on senaparib, a new parp inhibitor, as first-line maintenance in advanced high-grade ovarian cancer responding to platinum. The European Medicines Agency committee gave a positive opinion on September seventeenth, and the decision usually follows about nine weeks later, so around late November; that date is an estimate. The pivotal trial, with four hundred four patients and run in China, cut the risk of progression by fifty-seven percent versus placebo, with similar benefit with or without a mutation in the breast cancer susceptibility genes. Olaparib and niraparib already cover this setting, so the question is what it adds; serious adverse events were also more frequent, and overall survival data are not yet mature.

Third, the United States Food and Drug Administration is giving priority review to elinzanetant, a neurokinin one and three receptor antagonist, for moderate to severe hot flashes caused by endocrine therapy for hormone receptor positive breast cancer. Bayer announced the priority review on September twenty-eighth but did not publish the decision date; based on the six-month clock for that pathway, we estimate a decision between late twenty twenty-six and early twenty twenty-seven. Today these patients cannot use estrogen and rely on antidepressants, gabapentin or oxybutynin, with modest effect. The phase three trial, with four hundred seventy-four women, met its endpoints at four and twelve weeks, and the European Union has already approved this use. Critical take: the trial was run outside the United States, and patients should be warned about sleepiness, one of the most frequent side effects in the trial.

Fourth, the March twenty twenty-seven meetings. The Society of Gynecologic Oncology meets in Phoenix from March nineteenth to twenty-second, and the Society for Maternal-Fetal Medicine in National Harbor, near Washington, from March twenty-first to twenty-sixth. These dates are official; late-breaking studies have not been announced yet, and we will come back to them when the programs are out.
