Good morning, colleagues. This is the monthly infectious diseases bulletin from Boletines Médicos, for October fifteenth, two thousand twenty-six. We cover the past month, from September fifteenth to today. It was a month dominated by outbreak surveillance, from Central Africa to our own region.

We begin in Central Africa. The Ebola epidemic caused by Bundibugyo virus keeps growing in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. According to the World Health Organization report of October eighth, there are eight thousand seven hundred twenty-eight confirmed cases and four thousand two hundred five deaths. Case fatality is close to forty-eight percent. Sixty-four health zones in seven provinces are affected, and most cases are in Ituri. Kenya reported its first imported case, a traveler who came from the Congo through Uganda and died on October fifth. Contact follow-up is around eighty percent, below target. Uganda's outbreak, by contrast, has been declared over, and the international public health emergency declared in May remains in force. The critical point: there is no approved vaccine or treatment for this virus species, because the approved products are specific to the Zaire species, so everything depends on detection, isolation, and protecting staff. Risk to the Caribbean is very low, but hospitals should keep their protocol for febrile travelers from Central Africa up to date.

In our own region, the Pan American Health Organization rates the risk of measles spread in the Americas as very high, ahead of several mass religious gatherings. Between January fourth and October third there were fifty-five thousand seven hundred ninety-two confirmed cases and seventy-seven deaths in seventeen countries and territories. That is nearly five times last year's figure for the same period. Guatemala, Mexico, the United States, Peru, and Canada account for almost all cases. Coverage with the second dose was only eighty-five percent last year, and about one point six million children received no dose at all. The organization recommends a measles and rubella vaccine dose, ideally at least two weeks in advance, for anyone six months or older without proof of vaccination who travels or attends mass gatherings. The critical point: with that coverage and so much travel, no Caribbean country is safe from imported cases, so review staff vaccination and think of measles in febrile travelers with rash.

In the Dominican Republic, leptospirosis has nearly doubled. According to press coverage of the Ministry of Public Health surveillance bulletin, through epidemiological week thirty-eight there were two hundred seventy-nine confirmed cases and twenty deaths, compared with one hundred forty-two cases and thirteen deaths a year earlier. Pertussis also rose, from one case to eight. The critical point: in the rainy season, with fever, muscle pain, and exposure to water or rodents, empirical doxycycline or penicillin should not wait for serology.

In Europe, mosquitoes now transmit tropical viruses every summer. The European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control, in its weekly report for early October, counts one hundred forty-six locally acquired chikungunya cases in France and sixty-eight dengue cases in Italy. There are also one thousand eight hundred fifty-five local West Nile virus cases in eighteen countries, and Germany's first reported symptomatic locally acquired Sindbis virus case. The critical point: chikungunya and dengue are no longer only diagnoses for travelers returning from the tropics.

In human immunodeficiency virus care, a University of California, San Francisco study published October eighth describes forty-six patients treated with injectable lenacapavir and cabotegravir. Nearly three in four had resistance that limits use of the approved cabotegravir and rilpivirine combination, and more than half started with detectable viral load. All reached viral suppression within thirteen weeks, and after a median of almost two years all remained undetectable. The critical point: this is a single-center series, with no comparison group, using a combination not yet approved; the two announced trials are needed.

In healthcare-associated infection, the journal Eurosurveillance describes an outbreak of Ralstonia bloodstream infections in seven German hospitals. Sixteen genetically related cases, between March and September, were linked to prefilled saline flush syringes from a single manufacturer. The product was quarantined, but it had been shipped to other countries. The critical point: environmental bacteria in blood cultures from catheterized patients should prompt a search for a common contaminated product.

On the calendar, IDWeek takes place in Washington from October twenty-first to twenty-fourth. Industry has announced phase three data on a Lyme disease vaccine, data on a twenty-five valent pneumococcal vaccine in infants, and real-world data on its respiratory syncytial virus vaccine in nursing homes. The announcement includes no efficacy figures; we will review the results critically once presented.
