Good morning, colleagues. This issue covers September second to October second, twenty twenty-six, plus four trials from late August that earn their place. There are thirteen verified items. The month was shaped by the European diabetes meeting in Milan. The chronic disease section is short, and it is not padded.

We start with the Staree trial, in the New England Journal of Medicine. Nine thousand nine hundred seventy-one people aged seventy or older, without cardiovascular disease, received atorvastatin forty milligrams or placebo. Major cardiovascular events were six point zero versus eight point three percent, a hazard ratio of zero point seven zero. Dementia- and disability-free survival did not differ. The population was healthy: it should not be extrapolated to frail older adults with polypharmacy.

In the Triumph two trial, one thousand one hundred fifty-two adults with type two diabetes and obesity or overweight received weekly retatrutide or placebo. At eighty weeks, the highest dose achieved twenty point eight percent weight loss, versus four point zero with placebo. The figures use the efficacy estimand, the trial is manufacturer-run, and there are no cardiovascular outcomes yet.

The Achieve four trial compared oral orforglipron with insulin glargine in two thousand seven hundred forty-nine adults with type two diabetes. For major cardiovascular events, the hazard ratio was zero point eight four, meeting noninferiority. It establishes safety, not cardiovascular benefit. The mortality reduction highlighted in the press release should be read as hypothesis-generating.

On September twenty-fourth, the United States Food and Drug Administration approved insulin efsitora alfa, the first once-weekly basal insulin in the United States. Four trials showed noninferiority versus glargine and degludec, without superiority. The benefit is convenience, not efficacy. The practical risk lies in dosing errors and in the patient who is admitted or stops eating.

In intermediate-high-risk pulmonary embolism, the Czech Prague twenty-six trial randomized five hundred fifty-eight patients. It compared catheter-directed thrombolysis plus anticoagulation with anticoagulation alone. The seven-day composite outcome was zero point seven versus six point eight percent. The benefit is driven by hemodynamic decompensation, not mortality, and there were two intracranial hemorrhages in the catheter group.

In India, the Time heart failure trial tested nurse-coordinated care with a mobile application in one thousand five hundred seven adults. Survival without hospitalization at two years was eighty-four point zero versus seventy-nine point four percent. This is a low-cost model, more transferable to the Dominican Republic than a new drug.

The American and European infectious disease societies released seven consensus statements on Staphylococcus aureus bacteremia. They replace the complicated or uncomplicated classification with low and high risk. In low-risk patients, fourteen days of therapy. It is an initial consensus, and the risk is over-reading the fourteen days in high-risk patients.

Starting dapagliflozin during admission for heart failure gave slightly faster decongestion in two thousand four hundred one patients. At one week, body weight fell by an extra zero point six zero kilograms. The effects are small, and the trial's primary outcome was neutral. Generics exist, so it is applicable in our setting.

The Food and Drug Administration extended finerenone to chronic kidney disease associated with type one diabetes. In two hundred forty-two adults, it reduced the urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio by twenty-eight percent at month six. The approval rests on a surrogate outcome, and potassium must be monitored: hyperkalemia ten point one versus three point three percent.

Tozorakimab, an antibody against interleukin thirty-three, reduced exacerbations of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease in former smokers. The reductions were twenty-nine and thirty-four percent in the twin Oberon and Titania trials. But there are no mortality data, and it is not yet approved.

In the Dominican Republic, two hundred eighty-eight dengue cases had accumulated by epidemiological week thirty-five, versus two hundred nine in twenty twenty-five. Serotype two is circulating. With the rainy season, keep a low threshold for dengue testing. The figures come from national press reporting on the Ministry of Public Health bulletin.

In hepatitis delta, the antibody brelovitug met its primary endpoint in phase three. At week twenty-four, the combined response was fifty-six percent with the weekly dose, versus zero in the delayed-treatment group. This is a manufacturer press release, not peer reviewed, with a surrogate outcome.

Finally, according to Agência Brasil, the Brazilian health regulator registered generic liraglutide for obesity and type two diabetes. There are no new clinical data; this is an access story that may put pressure on prices across Latin America.
