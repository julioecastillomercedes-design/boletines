This edition of what's next in internal medicine covers what is due between November and December twenty twenty-six: three late-breaking sessions at the American Heart Association meeting, a pending regulatory decision in obesity, and the yearly update of the diabetes standards of care. For each one, I will tell you what clinical problem it addresses and how firm the date is.

First, lipoprotein little a and inflammation. On Saturday, November seventh, the full results of the HORIZON trial of pelacarsen, in more than eight thousand patients with established cardiovascular disease, will be presented. According to Novartis, it did not reduce major cardiovascular events even though it lowered lipoprotein little a. The same session features ZEUS, testing ziltivekimab, a monthly antibody against interleukin six. According to Novo Nordisk, in more than six thousand three hundred patients with atherosclerotic disease, chronic kidney disease and inflammation, it also failed to reduce events, with a hazard ratio of zero point nine nine and more serious infections. Today we have no specific therapy for high lipoprotein little a or for residual inflammatory risk. The subgroups and curves will draw attention, but secondary analyses of a negative trial generate hypotheses, not indications.

Second, the STREAM trial, also on November seventh. This publicly funded Swiss trial, with sites in France and the Netherlands, plans to enroll one thousand eight hundred people aged seventy or older, with at least two chronic conditions and no cardiovascular disease, randomly assigned to stop or continue their primary prevention statin. It aims to show non-inferiority for a composite of non-fatal heart attack, non-fatal ischemic stroke and death from any cause, analyzed over two years. We face this question in clinic every week and answer it today without randomized evidence. Note that the non-inferiority margin is a wide five absolute points, and the trial is open label.

Third, on Monday, November ninth, the SYNCHRONIZE cardiovascular outcomes trial of survodutide, a dual glucagon and glucagon-like peptide one receptor agonist from Boehringer Ingelheim. This double-blind trial randomized five thousand five hundred thirty-one adults with overweight or obesity plus cardiovascular disease, kidney disease or risk factors, against placebo. We need to know whether these new molecules are safe for the heart before wide use. It is designed mainly as a safety study, so a neutral result does not prove benefit.

Fourth, the United States Food and Drug Administration decision on CagriSema, the weekly combination of cagrilintide and semaglutide. Novo Nordisk filed it on December eighteenth, twenty twenty-five, and says the agency is expected to review the application in twenty twenty-six; no official decision date has been published. In the REDEFINE one trial, with three thousand four hundred seventeen adults with obesity or overweight and without diabetes, weight loss was twenty point four percent versus three percent with placebo at sixty-eight weeks. It would widen obesity options, but cost and gastrointestinal tolerance will matter greatly in our setting.

Fifth, the American Diabetes Association standards of care for twenty twenty-seven. The association publishes them every December, and the last two editions came out in December, so the date is an estimate. They are a practical reference for many internists; look for changes on obesity, kidney disease and new oral drugs, and check the committee's conflict-of-interest disclosures.
