Good morning, colleagues. This bulletin covers August thirtieth to September twenty-ninth, twenty twenty-six. It was a strong month for regulatory decisions, with two approvals from the United States Food and Drug Administration, and for glomerular disease. It was a thin one for dialysis and vascular access, where no new verifiable trial was found, and nothing was padded.

The Food and Drug Administration approved finerenone for chronic kidney disease associated with type one diabetes. The approval rests on the phase three Fine One trial of two hundred forty-two adults. The urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio fell twenty-eight percent at six months versus placebo. Hyperkalemia occurred in ten point one versus three point three percent. Critical reading: it is the first new therapy for this population in more than thirty years, but the endpoint is a surrogate. Follow-up is six months and the trial was funded by Bayer. Potassium monitoring is mandatory.

The agency also approved obinutuzumab for frequently relapsing or steroid-dependent childhood-onset idiopathic nephrotic syndrome. It is indicated in adults and children aged two and older in complete remission. In the Inshore trial versus mycophenolate mofetil, complete remission at week fifty-two was ninety-five versus seventy-three percent, according to Genentech. Critical reading: it is a manufacturer trial and the release gives no sample size. It offers less steroid exposure, but the cost of the biologic will limit its use in our setting.

In immunoglobulin A nephropathy, Vera Therapeutics reported final results of the Origin three trial, with four hundred twenty-eight patients. The annual estimated glomerular filtration rate slope was measured in milliliters per minute per one point seven three square meters. It was minus zero point six with atacicept versus minus five point six with placebo. Composite kidney progression had a hazard ratio of zero point two four. Critical reading: it is a manufacturer-funded press release without peer review. If confirmed, the effect on the slope is large.

In transplantation, The Lancet published the first case of a pig kidney used as a bridge to human transplantation. A sixty-six-year-old man remained dialysis-free for two hundred seventy-one days. The graft failed after immunosuppression was reduced because of an infection. He then received a deceased-donor kidney with immediate function and no sensitization. Critical reading: it is a single case under expanded access. Still, it shows that a xenograft can buy time without compromising the later transplant.

Boehringer Ingelheim stopped the phase three Easy Kidney trial for futility. It compared vicadrostat plus empagliflozin with empagliflozin alone in adults with chronic kidney disease. The release gives no numbers or safety findings. Critical reading: only the headline is available, with no peer review. The lesson is that adding a fourth pillar on top of a sodium-glucose cotransporter two inhibitor does not guarantee benefit.

A Bayesian network meta-analysis in Frontiers in Pharmacology pooled twenty thousand five hundred twenty-one participants with type two diabetes and chronic kidney disease. Versus placebo, the composite kidney outcome fell with sodium-glucose cotransporter two inhibitors, hazard ratio zero point seven zero. It was zero point seven six with semaglutide and zero point eight four with finerenone. Critical reading: these are indirect comparisons from few trials, and they do not replace a head-to-head trial.

Vertex reported a single-arm phase two b study of inaxaplin in apolipoprotein L one mediated kidney disease. It included forty-one patients over thirteen weeks. In the cohort without diabetes, albuminuria fell forty-two point seven percent. In the cohort with type two diabetes it fell seventeen point three percent, not significant. Critical reading: there is no control group and the endpoint is a surrogate. Still, it is a genetic target in a disease with potentially high prevalence in the Caribbean.

The European Society of Cardiology and the European Renal Association published on August twenty-ninth their first joint guideline on cardiovascular disease in chronic kidney disease. The American Society of Nephrology launched a glomerular disease compendium. Critical reading: both are consensus documents rather than new evidence.

Regionally, the Brazilian Society of Nephrology held its congress on September sixteenth to nineteenth in Belo Horizonte. In China, the national essential medicines list took effect, adding finerenone, roxadustat and sevelamer. Critical reading: it is health policy, not evidence, but it shows how large systems bring kidney protection into public access.

Looking ahead, the American Society of Nephrology Kidney Week runs October twenty-second to twenty-fifth in Denver. The Spanish Society of Nephrology congress takes place November fifth to seventh in Murcia.

Thank you for listening. Until next month.
