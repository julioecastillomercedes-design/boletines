Good morning, colleagues. This summary covers September eighth to October eighth, twenty twenty-six. In dialysis and vascular access we found no verifiable randomized trials within the window. We gathered eleven verified items.

As Bayer announced on September sixteenth, the United States Food and Drug Administration extended finerenone, Kerendia, to chronic kidney disease associated with type one diabetes. The basis is the double-blind Fine One trial in two hundred forty-two adults. The urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio fell twenty-eight percent more than with placebo at month six. Hyperkalemia was ten point one versus three point three percent. Critical reading: according to Bayer, it is the first new treatment in more than thirty years for this group. But approval rests on a surrogate endpoint at six months, without hard kidney outcomes, and potassium monitoring is required.

Vera Therapeutics reported on September fifteenth the final analysis of the Origin three trial, in four hundred twenty-eight patients with immunoglobulin A nephropathy. With atacicept, the two-year slope of glomerular filtration was minus zero point six. With placebo it was minus five point six milliliters per minute per year. Kidney progression events were eleven versus thirty-eight, with a hazard ratio of zero point two four. Critical reading: it provides two-year kidney function data, but these are still press-release data without peer-reviewed publication.

On September twenty-fifth, the agency approved obinutuzumab to prevent relapses in childhood-onset idiopathic nephrotic syndrome. The Inshore trial enrolled eighty-five patients aged two to twenty-five. Sustained complete remission at week fifty-two was ninety-five point five versus seventy-three point two percent with mycophenolate. Critical reading: it is the first new therapy in decades and spares steroids, but the trial is small, with one-year follow-up and high cost.

Japan's regulator recorded on September sixteenth the approval of iptacopan, Fabhalta, for adult immunoglobulin A nephropathy. It is based on the Applause trial. According to the company, it reduced the risk of progression to kidney failure by forty-three percent. Critical reading: Japan has one of the highest prevalences of this disease, but the figures come from the manufacturer and should be checked against the final publication.

In transplantation, The Lancet published on September third, outside the period, the Massachusetts General Hospital case. A sixty-six-year-old man lived two hundred seventy-one days off dialysis with a gene-edited pig kidney. As immunosuppression was reduced during an infection, the xenograft progressed to failure. In January twenty twenty-six he received a deceased-donor kidney with immediate function. Critical reading: it is a single case, but it shows for the first time that xenotransplantation can serve as a bridge.

At the international transplantation congress in Sydney, Eledon presented the extension of the phase two Bestow study, with eighty-one patients at twenty-four months. Mean glomerular filtration was seventy-one with tegoprubart versus fifty-eight with tacrolimus. Critical reading: these are manufacturer data in a press release, phase two, with cohort attrition. Practice does not change until the Legacy trial confirms safety and non-inferiority.

The American Journal of Kidney Diseases published a target trial emulation in patients aged seventy-five or older. Deceased-donor transplantation carried higher mortality in the first months. But the survival advantage became evident after about three years. Critical reading: it is an observational design with risk of residual confounding. Still, it supports not excluding patients from the waitlist on age alone.

A survey of sixty-one United States nephrology providers, published in Kidney Medicine, identified cost as the top barrier. It was cited for glucagon-like peptide one receptor agonists by eighty-four percent. For sodium-glucose cotransporter two inhibitors, the figure was seventy-four percent. Critical reading: the sample is small and self-selected. Still, it confirms that the gap between evidence and prescribing is economic, a problem even larger in our setting.

Reports based on Texas Monthly describe healthy men in their twenties and thirties arriving with kidney failure at Houston safety-net hospitals. Most are migrant workers from Mexico and Central America, without diabetes or hypertension. About one in six emergency dialysis cases among uninsured or undocumented patients had no identifiable cause. Critical reading: this is journalism, not a peer-reviewed study. But it reinforces the link between heat, physical labor and kidney disease of nontraditional cause.

The American Journal of Kidney Diseases also describes an artificial intelligence virtual nephrologist that simulates a video consultation about transplantation. After using it, participants reported greater willingness to discuss transplantation with their doctor. Critical reading: the outcome is self-reported with no control group described; it has yet to show more actual transplants.

Finally, Akebia announced that the Voice trial will be presented on October twenty-third at Kidney Week in Denver. It compares the safety of three-times-weekly vadadustat with an erythropoiesis-stimulating agent in hemodialysis. No results have been disclosed. Critical reading: it is only an announcement, but the result will determine whether hypoxia-inducible factor prolyl hydroxylase inhibitors gain ground in dialysis anemia.
