This is the issue on what is coming in nephrology over the next six months, from the third week of October two thousand twenty-six to April two thousand twenty-seven. There are six milestones with verifiable dates: two presentations at the American Society of Nephrology meeting, a guideline in public review, two regulatory decisions and an expected interim analysis.

First, the ORIGIN three trial of atacicept in immunoglobulin A nephropathy. Its final analysis is presented on October twenty-second at the opening plenary of Kidney Week in Denver. According to the company, in four hundred twenty-eight patients the loss of filtration over two years was six tenths of a milliliter per minute per year on the drug, versus five point six on placebo, and the risk of progression fell by seventy-six percent. The drug already holds accelerated approval in the United States based on proteinuria. Today, despite renin angiotensin blockade, sodium glucose cotransporter two inhibitors and budesonide, many patients keep losing function. Critical take: these are sponsor figures without peer review, filtration slope is a surrogate endpoint, and hard events were few.

Second, upacicalcet, an investigational intravenous calcimimetic for secondary hyperparathyroidism on hemodialysis. The placebo-controlled phase three trials PATH one and PATH two are presented on October twenty-third at the same meeting. Today we rely on oral cinacalcet, with nausea and poor adherence, on etelcalcetide, active vitamin D and surgery. Critical take: the comparator is placebo, the expected endpoint is lower parathyroid hormone, with no data on fractures or mortality, and the results are not yet public.

Third, the focused update of the international chronic kidney disease guideline from the Kidney Disease Improving Global Outcomes initiative. The draft, which reviews sodium glucose cotransporter two inhibitors, glucagon-like peptide one based therapies and nonsteroidal mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, is open for public review until November fourth, two thousand twenty-six. No date has been announced for the final version. It aims to clarify when and how to combine three drug classes that remain underused. Critical take: it is a draft that may change, and uptake in our setting will depend on cost and coverage.

Fourth, povetacicept, a dual inhibitor of two cytokines that activate B cells, in immunoglobulin A nephropathy. The United States drug agency will decide on its accelerated approval on November thirtieth, two thousand twenty-six. In the RAINIER trial, with six hundred five adults, proteinuria fell by almost fifty percent versus placebo at thirty-six weeks. It would be a second drug in this class. Critical take: the decision rests on proteinuria, a surrogate endpoint, and the effect on filtration at two years is not yet known.

Fifth, imlifidase, an enzyme that cleaves immunoglobulin G and allows transplantation of highly sensitized patients with a positive crossmatch. The United States agency decision is set for December nineteenth, two thousand twenty-six. In the ConfIdeS trial, with sixty-four patients, filtration at one year was fifty-one point five versus nineteen point three in the control group. Today these patients wait years, with off-label desensitization of limited efficacy. Critical take: it is a small open-label trial whose control group included staying on dialysis, and it requires a transplant program with round-the-clock immunology.

Sixth, inaxaplin in kidney disease mediated by the APOL one gene. The company expects the interim analysis of the AMPLITUDE trial in early two thousand twenty-seven, and it could support accelerated approval. This disease explains much of the excess kidney disease in people of African ancestry and has no specific treatment today. Critical take: the interim analysis will likely rely on proteinuria, and use will require genotyping, which is scarce in the region, although the topic is highly relevant to Caribbean populations.
