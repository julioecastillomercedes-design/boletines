Good morning, colleagues. This issue covers August twenty-eighth to September twenty-eighth, two thousand twenty-six. The month was dominated by the European Respiratory Society congress in Barcelona. The issue holds sixteen items; here we review the fourteen with the most weight.

Tozorakimab, an anti-interleukin thirty-three antibody, reduced chronic obstructive pulmonary disease exacerbations in the phase three Oberon and Titania trials. In former smokers, the reduction was twenty-nine and thirty-four percent versus placebo. The effect held with low eosinophils. A slight numerical imbalance in major cardiovascular events and deaths requires monitoring. It is not approved in any country.

The United States Food and Drug Administration added Hyperion trial data to the sotatercept label. The trial enrolled three hundred twenty adults with recently diagnosed pulmonary arterial hypertension. The hazard ratio for clinical worsening was zero point two four. It supports early use, but the trial stopped early, which may overstate the effect.

Also in pulmonary arterial hypertension, the Advance Outcomes trial tested oral ralinepag in six hundred eighty-seven patients. The hazard ratio for clinical worsening was zero point four five. Six-minute walk distance improved by twenty point four meters. The analysis is the company's and is not yet peer-reviewed.

A pooled analysis of the Danflu two and Galflu trials assessed high-dose influenza vaccine in thirty-one thousand nine hundred seventy-six adults over sixty-five with chronic lung disease. High dose cut influenza or pneumonia hospitalizations by eight point eight percent. It is a subgroup, and the release gives no confidence intervals.

The European Commission approved the triple combination of budesonide, glycopyrronium and formoterol in a single inhaler for asthma from age twelve. It rests on the Kalos and Logos trials, with about four thousand three hundred patients. The release gives no absolute magnitudes or intervals, and the comparator was a medium dose, not a high dose.

The Danish Copernicos trial tested eosinophil-guided inhaled corticosteroid withdrawal in severe chronic obstructive pulmonary disease. Withdrawal did not significantly increase severe exacerbations or mortality. But the non-inferiority margin was not formally met. The trial was underpowered, and the full paper is worth waiting for.

A Bayesian meta-analysis of one hundred ten trials and twenty-two thousand six hundred eight patients assessed mortality with cefepime. The odds ratio was one point one zero, with a credible interval of zero point nine eight to one point two four. The probability of harm was ninety-four point four percent. The effect is small and heterogeneity is high.

The Journal of the American Medical Association published three systematic reviews of vaccines for this season. Against respiratory syncytial virus, effectiveness against hospitalization in adults over sixty was eighty-three point three percent in the first season. It fell to forty-eight point five percent at twelve to eighteen months. Heterogeneity of the observational studies limits the synthesis.

A British cohort of people with diabetes linked semaglutide to fewer asthma attacks, up to forty percent fewer according to the European Respiratory Society. None of the other three agonists showed a significant association. It is observational and not peer-reviewed. It does not justify prescribing semaglutide for the airways.

The agency granted priority review to amikacin liposome inhalation suspension as first-line therapy for Mycobacterium avium complex lung disease. The decision date is January twenty-eighth, two thousand twenty-seven. The Encore trial enrolled four hundred twenty-five patients. The company has not disclosed the effect size, and toxicity is considerable.

The agency accepted the application for inhaled treprostinil in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. In the Teton trials, the difference in forced vital capacity was one hundred eleven point eight milliliters at fifty-two weeks. It is a surrogate endpoint, and the figures come from the company's release.

The Spray see bee trial compared bronchoscopic cryospray with a sham procedure in two hundred three patients with chronic bronchitis. The Saint George's Respiratory Questionnaire improved by five point nine points at twelve months. There were eight deaths, six versus two, none attributed to the device. The effect is at the edge of significance.

In critical care, the Wean Safe cohort studied weaning failure from mechanical ventilation. Fifteen point one percent of patients with at least one separation attempt met the criteria, with intensive care mortality of seventy-eight percent. The first separation attempt emerges as a critical moment. It is observational, with no causal inference.

We close in Brazil. The Brelt three study took mobile low-dose computed tomography screening to the country's northeast. Two thousand eighteen people were screened and nineteen cancers were diagnosed, a detection rate of zero point nine four percent. Without a comparator or mortality data, it serves as an implementation model for the Caribbean.
