In this edition of What's next in pulmonology we review three pending regulatory decisions in the United States between January and late April twenty twenty-seven: one on mycobacteria, one on a new biologic pathway in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, and an application that could change how we treat idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis.

First, amikacin liposome inhalation suspension for Mycobacterium avium complex lung disease. In the United States it is currently limited to refractory disease, after six months of multidrug therapy without culture conversion, under accelerated approval. The ENCORE trial, randomized, double-blind and placebo-controlled, with four hundred twenty-five patients with new or recurrent, previously untreated infection, compared the drug or placebo added to azithromycin and ethambutol for twelve months, and according to the company it met its primary endpoint, improvement in respiratory symptoms at month thirteen, as well as culture conversion. The United States Food and Drug Administration granted priority review, with a decision date of January twenty-eighth, twenty twenty-seven. Critical take: the trial is company-sponsored, the release does not give effect sizes, and cost and tolerability, including dysphonia, cough and bronchospasm, will matter a great deal in our setting.

Second, tozorakimab, an antibody against interleukin thirty-three, for chronic obstructive pulmonary disease with exacerbations. The OBERON and TITANIA trials, with two thousand three hundred six patients, cut moderate or severe exacerbations by twenty-nine to thirty-four percent in former smokers and by twenty-nine to thirty percent overall, with an effect also below one hundred fifty eosinophils. That matters because current biologics mainly target the eosinophilic phenotype. A third trial, MIRANDA, was also positive according to the company, although no figures were released. The agency accepted the application under priority review and the company expects a decision in the first quarter of twenty twenty-seven; it is also under review in Europe and China. Critical take: moderate relative reductions, a smaller benefit with low eosinophils, sponsor-funded trials, and a price that will likely limit access in our region.

Third, nebulized treprostinil in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. Today we have nintedanib, pirfenidone and, more recently, nerandomilast, which slow but do not stop the decline in forced vital capacity. In the TETON two trial, with five hundred ninety-seven patients, three in four on background antifibrotics, the difference versus placebo at fifty-two weeks was about ninety-six milliliters; the pooled analysis with TETON one was about one hundred twelve milliliters. The company expects the review to be completed in late April twenty twenty-seven. Critical take: forced vital capacity is a surrogate endpoint, there are no survival data yet, and nebulizing several times a day is a real burden for patients.

In short, the coming months bring decisions on mycobacteria, on a new biologic pathway in chronic obstructive disease, and on a possible new antifibrotic option. We will report the results as they arrive.
