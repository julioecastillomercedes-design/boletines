Good morning, colleagues. This newscast covers August twenty-ninth to September twenty-eighth, two thousand twenty-six. The month was dominated by regulatory decisions.

In the United States, the Greater Cincinnati Northern Kentucky Stroke Study analyzed two thousand seventy-six first-ever strokes in people aged twenty to fifty-four. Incidence rose from thirty-three point nine to sixty-two point two per one hundred thousand person-years between nineteen ninety-three and twenty twenty. Documented substance use among cases rose from four point six to forty point two percent. Critical reading: this is single-region trend data, so the rise cannot be attributed to any one factor. Still, it supports screening young adults for hypertension, diabetes and substance use.

The United States drug agency granted priority review to satralizumab for myelin oligodendrocyte glycoprotein antibody-associated disease. A decision is expected by January tenth, two thousand twenty-seven. In the Meteoroid trial, according to the manufacturer's release, relapse risk fell sixty-eight percent versus placebo. At forty-eight weeks, eighty-seven versus sixty-seven percent were relapse-free. Critical reading: it would be the first approved drug for this disease, but full data are not yet published.

Ionis and Otsuka announced that the Fusion trial of the intrathecal antisense oligonucleotide ulefnersen met its primary endpoint versus placebo. It enrolled seventy-three patients with amyotrophic lateral sclerosis due to a fus mutation, over seventy-two weeks. Critical reading: this is a company release with no effect size or adverse-event rates. It also applies only to a very rare variant that requires genetic testing, which is scarce in the Caribbean.

The United States drug agency approved apitegromab for spinal muscular atrophy in patients aged two and older already on targeted therapy. In the Sapphire trial, the difference on the Hammersmith Functional Motor Scale Expanded was two point two points with ten milligrams per kilogram. The label warns of more fractures: nine percent versus two percent with placebo. Critical reading: it is the first muscle-targeted therapy, with a modest benefit and added cost on top of already expensive therapies.

The Brazilian health agency approved atogepant, an oral gepant, for migraine prevention. In the Advance trial it reduced monthly migraine days by four point one, versus two point five with placebo. Critical reading: the absolute benefit is modest, and the price, still pending, will decide real access.

Xenon Pharmaceuticals filed azetukalner, a potassium channel opener, with the United States drug agency for focal seizures. According to the company, all four doses studied significantly reduced seizure frequency versus placebo. Critical reading: the release gives no figures, and tolerability and the lack of head-to-head data against cenobamate are open questions.

On September sixteenth, Japan approved the two hundred fifty milligram subcutaneous lecanemab pen for early Alzheimer disease. Critical reading: it reduces infusion burden, but not the need for magnetic resonance monitoring for amyloid-related imaging abnormalities.

In the same package, Japan approved cenobamate for partial-onset seizures, intravenous eptinezumab for migraine prevention, and pitolisant. Critical reading: these drugs are already known in other markets, with no new data, but they confirm uptake in Asia.

A study in The Lancet Neurology measured plasma proteins in one thousand nine hundred sixty-seven participants, one hundred ninety-seven of them on lecanemab. Thirty-four proteins changed with the number of infusions, in different directions. Critical reading: it is observational, exploratory, and publicized by the assay maker. It suggests that amyloid clearance is not the same as slowing neurodegeneration.

The European medicines committee recommended ocrelizumab for relapsing multiple sclerosis from age ten. In the Operetta Two trial, the annualized relapse rate was forty-eight percent lower than with fingolimod. Critical reading: the confidence interval on the clinical endpoint is wide, and the firmest support is radiological.

The United States drug agency approved zilganersen, the first treatment for Alexander disease. In forty-nine patients, walking speed at sixty-one weeks was significantly better in patients aged five and older with measurable walking difficulty. Critical reading: the control was no treatment rather than placebo, and effect sizes were not released.

In China, the Cable trial of Yangxue Qingnao pills in one hundred fourteen patients with small vessel disease did not improve global cognition at six months. Executive function did improve. Critical reading: the trial is small, unpublished and negative on its primary endpoint; the executive-function signal is only hypothesis-generating.

Finally, experts in Buenos Aires discussed implementing LatAm Fingers, a trial with a site in the Dominican Republic. It included one thousand sixty-five adults aged sixty to seventy-seven at risk of dementia, followed for two years. According to Infobae's coverage, cognitive improvements in the structured multidomain group were fifty-five percent greater. Critical reading: the paper predates the window, but the intervention is simple to replicate in primary care. Thank you for listening. Until next month.
