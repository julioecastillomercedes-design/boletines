This edition of what's next in oncology covers five dated milestones between mid November and late December twenty twenty-six: four decisions by the United States Food and Drug Administration and one breast cancer meeting. These are target review dates, not approvals; the agency can act early, extend the review, or decline to approve.

First, ivonescimab, a bispecific antibody against PD one and vascular endothelial growth factor, given with carboplatin and pemetrexed in non squamous non small cell lung cancer with an epidermal growth factor receptor mutation that progressed on a tyrosine kinase inhibitor. The target date is November fourteenth. In the double blind phase three HARMONi trial, with four hundred thirty-eight randomized patients, progression free survival improved with a hazard ratio of zero point five two, with medians of six point eight versus four point four months. But overall survival, a co-primary endpoint, did not reach statistical significance in the primary analysis, with a hazard ratio of zero point seven nine. With longer follow-up the hazard ratio moved to zero point seven six, on a descriptive basis only, and in Western patients the primary analysis showed no clear difference. After osimertinib, platinum chemotherapy is today's usual option. The question for the agency is whether the progression benefit is enough.

Second, neladalkib, a selective inhibitor of anaplastic lymphoma kinase designed to cross the blood brain barrier and cover resistance mutations such as G twelve oh two R. It is under priority review for lung cancer with that alteration previously treated with targeted inhibitors, with a target date of November twenty-seventh. The clinical gap is real: after lorlatinib, targeted options are scarce. The evidence comes from an open label, single arm phase one and two study, with response rate as the main endpoint and no randomized comparator.

Third, giredestrant, an oral selective estrogen receptor degrader, as adjuvant therapy for estrogen receptor positive, HER two negative breast cancer, stages one to three at intermediate or high risk. Target date: November thirtieth, under priority review. The open label lidERA trial, with more than four thousand one hundred patients, compared it with standard endocrine therapy: at the interim analysis, invasive disease free survival improved with a hazard ratio of zero point seven zero, ninety-two point four versus eighty-nine point six percent at three years. Overall survival is still immature. According to Roche, it is the first oral degrader with positive phase three results in the curative setting, where we now use tamoxifen or aromatase inhibitors for years.

Fourth, anitocabtagene autoleucel, a chimeric antigen receptor T cell therapy directed at B cell maturation antigen for relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma after at least three prior lines. Target date: December twenty-third. In the single arm phase two iMMagine one study, with one hundred seventeen treated patients, the company reports an overall response rate of ninety-six percent and complete or stringent complete response in seventy-four percent. In the analysis presented in December twenty twenty-five, no delayed neurotoxicity, including parkinsonism, was reported, a known concern with other therapies against that same antigen. It is a study without a comparator, and access to cell therapy remains limited in our region.

Fifth, the San Antonio Breast Cancer Symposium, December eighth to eleventh in Texas. It is an annual meeting dedicated to new data in hormone receptor positive disease, targeted therapy and antibody drug conjugates, and it often shapes the following year's guideline discussions. Read the abstracts carefully before changing practice.
