Good morning, colleagues. This is the orthopedics and trauma bulletin for the period from August thirtieth to September twenty-ninth, twenty twenty-six. It was a thin month for clinical data we could confirm at the original source. Several items come from manufacturer press releases. We publish five verified items rather than filler.

We start with osteoporosis. A first-in-human phase one trial published in Cell treated ten women aged fifty-one to seventy-two. They had severe osteoporosis and prior fractures. Each received a single intravenous infusion of autologous mesenchymal stromal cells engineered to bind marrow E-selectin. Fragility fractures fell from eight to zero point five per year over the first two years. That is ninety-four percent fewer. Bone density on densitometry did not improve at the group level. No serious adverse events were attributed to the infusion, over a median follow-up of about six years. The critical reading: ten patients, no control group, and most were on concomitant conventional therapy with medication changes. One of the authors also holds intellectual property rights on the technology. It is an interesting biological signal, not a treatment option, and a randomized trial is needed.

In sports medicine, a retrospective cohort study published in Sports Health on August twenty-seventh analyzed twenty-six thousand five hundred twenty anterior cruciate ligament reconstructions. All were in women aged thirteen to forty-nine. Those using oral contraceptives at the time of surgery had a twenty-five percent lower risk of repeat ipsilateral reconstruction at five years. The risk ratio was zero point seven five. The abstract does not report confidence intervals. The critical reading: this is an observational database association, open to confounding by activity level, age and adherence. It raises a hormonal hypothesis about graft maturation, but it does not justify prescribing contraceptives for orthopedic purposes.

In arthroplasty, Smith and Nephew announced on September twenty-second that the United States Food and Drug Administration cleared its Landmark total knee system. It has a fully three-dimensionally printed femoral component. It is designed to work with the Cori handheld robot. The company plans a phased launch, with broad availability through twenty twenty-seven. The critical reading: this is a manufacturer press release with no published clinical data, and the quoted surgeons are implant designers. It does not change practice, and availability in the Dominican Republic is not imminent.

In spine surgery, Bone Biologics announced on September eighth that it doubled to six the Australian hospitals in its pilot trial of N B One. It combines a recombinant human osteoinductive protein with demineralized bone matrix. The trial is multicenter, prospective and randomized, in up to thirty adults with single-level degenerative disc disease. All undergo transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion. Endpoints are fusion at twelve and twenty-four months and change in the Oswestry Disability Index. The company expects to complete enrollment by the end of twenty twenty-six. The critical reading: this is an enrollment update with no results, from a small company, with an unspecified comparator. It is of interest as a possible orthobiologic alternative, but there are no clinical data yet.

In pediatric orthopedics, OrthoPediatrics reported on September twenty-second that it has passed one thousand implants of its rigid pediatric tibial nail. It comes in diameters of seven to twelve millimeters, with physis-sparing fixation. Nearly twenty percent were seven-millimeter nails. The system is available in five markets, with a European launch planned for twenty twenty-seven. The critical reading: this is a commercial milestone with no healing, limb-length or complication data. Rigid nailing in adolescents with open physes still needs its own safety data.

What we did not find. This month we could not confirm at source any new clinical study in primary or revision arthroplasty or periprosthetic joint infection. Nor did we find trials with numbers in hip fracture, fracture fixation or polytrauma. There is coverage of a randomized trial comparing intramedullary nailing with dual-mobility total hip arthroplasty for pertrochanteric fracture. We could not access its data, so it is not included. There were no verifiable studies on hip dysplasia, scoliosis or rehabilitation. We searched in eight languages. Apart from the Australian trial, we found no verifiable items from Asia, Russia, the Middle East, Latin America or the Dominican Republic.

Thank you for listening. Until next month.
