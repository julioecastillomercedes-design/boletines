Good morning, colleagues. This summary covers August twenty-ninth to September twenty-eighth, twenty twenty-six. We gathered fifteen verified items. In neonatology there was no new verifiable randomized trial, so that section relies on surveillance data.

The United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention published eight years of the Champs network in seven countries of sub-Saharan Africa and Bangladesh. Eight thousand five hundred deaths were investigated in depth. Prematurity, perinatal asphyxia and sepsis dominated neonatal deaths. Experts judged eighty point seven percent of the assessed deaths preventable or possibly preventable. Critical reading: the sites are not representative and preventability is an expert judgment. The lesson is the weight of multidrug-resistant gram-negative sepsis and intrapartum asphyxia.

In the Dominican Republic, the official epidemiological bulletin records one thousand three hundred forty-nine infant deaths this year. The same period last year had one thousand three hundred seventy-two. Neonatal deaths account for eighty-one point five percent. Critical reading: the reduction is marginal, and gains must now come from delivery care, neonatal resuscitation, and management of prematurity and sepsis.

The American Academy of Pediatrics published its respiratory syncytial virus policy. For the second season, ages eight to nineteen months, it expands high risk to infants born before thirty-two weeks, significant heart disease, neuromuscular disease and Down syndrome. Critical reading: the expansion relies on risk extrapolation, not trials, and these products are not in our public schedule.

The same academy recommends covid vaccination for all children aged six to twenty-three months, and from two to eighteen years for those at higher risk of severe disease. Critical reading: it departs from current federal recommendations, and effectiveness data are observational.

A federal survey shows that only sixty-three point four percent of United States adolescents completed human papillomavirus vaccination, unchanged since twenty twenty-one. Critical reading: the response rate was twenty-one percent and may underestimate coverage by about five points, but the plateau is real.

The Dominican bulletin reports three hundred thirty-eight dengue cases and one death, fifty percent more than in twenty twenty-five, though below the alarm threshold. Pertussis rose from one to eight cases. Critical reading: absolute numbers are low, but they call for reviewing pentavalent coverage and suspecting pertussis in infants with paroxysmal cough.

Novo Nordisk reported topline results of the Step Young trial in one hundred sixty-five children aged six to under twelve with obesity. With semaglutide, forty point four percent fell below the ninety-fifth percentile, versus zero percent with placebo. Critical reading: this is a manufacturer press release, without the primary endpoint magnitude or adverse events.

In a trial of eighty-one children with hypochondroplasia, vosoritide increased growth velocity by two point three three centimeters per year over placebo, with no severe adverse events. Critical reading: it is a one-year, manufacturer-funded trial with no adult height data.

Suicide-related emergency visits among United States children aged five to seventeen rose seventy-four percent between twenty sixteen and twenty twenty-two. About half occurred in departments with low pediatric volume. Critical reading: these are visit counts, not children, but any emergency department must be ready to screen for risk.

Lilly announced United States approval of baricitinib for severe alopecia areata at ages twelve to seventeen. At least eighty percent scalp coverage was reached by forty-two percent with four milligrams, versus five percent with placebo. Critical reading: safety follow-up in adolescents is short, and prescribing should stay with dermatology.

Outside the period, Amgen and AstraZeneca reported that tezepelumab met the co-primary endpoints of the Crossing trial in eosinophilic esophagitis. Critical reading: no efficacy or adverse event figures have been published.

The Food and Drug Administration approved obinutuzumab for childhood-onset idiopathic nephrotic syndrome that is frequently relapsing or steroid-dependent. In the open-label Inshore trial, with eighty-five patients, complete remission at week fifty-two was ninety-five versus seventy-three percent with mycophenolate, according to Genentech. Critical reading: the trial is small and open-label, and rituximab remains the realistic option in our setting.

Apitegromab was approved for spinal muscular atrophy, as an add-on to nusinersen or risdiplam. It improved the Hammersmith expanded motor scale by two point two points over placebo at one year. Critical reading: the benefit is modest but real, and the primary analysis excluded patients over twelve.

Fayuvi was approved as the first gene therapy for Sanfilippo syndrome type A. Treated children maintained or improved cognitive function compared with a historical cohort. Critical reading: there was no concurrent control group, and it strengthens the case for expanded newborn screening.

Finally, zilganersen was approved as the first treatment for Alexander disease. According to Ionis, walking speed was thirty-three point three percent better than with control. Critical reading: the sample is tiny, and extrapolation to infants is the weakest part. Thank you for listening. Until next month.
