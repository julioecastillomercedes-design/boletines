Good morning, colleagues. This issue covers September sixth to October sixth, two thousand twenty-six. It was a solid month, with thirteen verified items. Several regulatory data points come from secondary coverage, because primary sources blocked access.

The American Academy of Pediatrics published its new urinary tract infection guideline. It extends the age range to eight days through five years and removes race as a risk factor. It prefers courses of seven days or fewer for most children. It recommends evaluation within seventy-two hours for children aged two months to five years with fever without a source. The academy itself asks for local adaptation, given access to urine culture, ultrasound and resistance patterns. Exact thresholds must be read in the original.

In neonatology, the Australian Airoplane trial compared starting with thirty percent oxygen versus air. It analyzed one thousand eight hundred eighteen newborns of thirty-two to thirty-five weeks. Ongoing respiratory support on leaving the delivery room was seventy-two point six versus seventy-three point three percent. The difference was not significant. There is no basis for routinely starting at thirty percent.

In intensive care, the Pressure trial randomized one thousand nine hundred children with hypotension. It compared a permissive mean arterial pressure target above the fifth percentile with usual care. There was no difference in mortality and ventilation duration at thirty days. Total vasoactive dose was thirty-seven point five percent lower. It does not show superiority, and the control arm was usual care, not a fixed high target.

Measles in the Americas. The Pan American Health Organization reports fifty-three thousand nine hundred fifty-seven confirmed cases and sixty-eight deaths in two thousand twenty-six. Guatemala has thirty-three thousand eight hundred sixty-six cases and Mexico twelve thousand nine hundred forty-three. The report mentions no cases in the Dominican Republic, but the risk of importation is real. Check two doses of measles, mumps and rubella vaccine at every visit.

In the Dominican Republic, the week thirty-six bulletin, as reported by the press, records three hundred thirty-eight dengue cases, versus two hundred twenty in two thousand twenty-five. Pertussis rose from one to eight cases. Absolute numbers are low, so percentages exaggerate. The actionable point is rising pertussis: review vaccination in pregnancy and the infant schedule.

In Bangladesh, according to the Directorate General of Health Services as cited by a news agency, the measles outbreak has reached one thousand sixty-three deaths. This is not a World Health Organization report, and the count mixes confirmed and suspected deaths. It should be read as an order of magnitude. Even so, it shows the cost of falling coverage.

The Champs network, in sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia, analyzed eight thousand five hundred stillbirths and deaths under age five. Eighty point seven percent of those assessed were preventable or possibly preventable. Klebsiella pneumoniae was present in forty-eight point three percent of neonatal deaths with a pathogen in the causal chain. Catchment areas are not representative of each country.

The United States Food and Drug Administration approved obinutuzumab for childhood-onset idiopathic nephrotic syndrome. In the Inshore trial of eighty-five patients, sustained remission at one year was ninety-five point five versus seventy-three point two percent with mycophenolate. Severe adverse events were twenty-five versus seven point three percent. There is no head-to-head comparison with rituximab.

It also approved apitegromab for spinal muscular atrophy, added to background therapy from age two. The Hammersmith Functional Motor Scale Expanded improved by two point two points versus placebo at one year. There is a warning for fracture risk. The gain is modest, and the data come from the manufacturer.

The agency extended mavacamten to adolescents with obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy. According to the manufacturer, in forty-four adolescents the Valsalva gradient fell by forty-nine point four millimeters of mercury, versus one point eight with placebo. Patients are few, and the endpoint is hemodynamic, not clinical.

Novo Nordisk reported the Step Young trial of semaglutide in one hundred sixty-five children aged six to eleven with obesity. At sixty-eight weeks, forty point four percent were below the obesity threshold, versus zero percent with placebo. The release omits the primary endpoint figure and adverse event rates.

An application was submitted to the agency for the epicutaneous peanut patch in children aged four to seven. In the Vitesse trial, forty-six point six percent responded with the patch, versus fourteen point eight with placebo. It is an application, not an approval, and the data come from the sponsor.

Finally, China announced market exclusivity for pediatric and rare disease medicines. It is a policy announcement, with no timelines or published regulation, and no immediate practical effect.
