Good morning, colleagues. This is the pediatrics bulletin for October thirteenth, twenty twenty-six. It covers the week from October seventh through October thirteenth, plus five stories from the preceding weeks. There is no news in neonatology this time.

We begin with measles. The Pan American Health Organization now rates the risk of spread in the Americas as very high. Between January fourth and October third, seventeen countries and territories confirmed fifty-five thousand seven hundred ninety-two cases and seventy-seven deaths. That is a four point eight-fold increase over the same period last year. Guatemala, Mexico, the United States, Peru, and Canada account for ninety-nine point five percent of cases. Regional first-dose coverage was eighty-eight percent, well short of the ninety-five percent needed, so this is the moment to check records.

Next, the most contested story of the week. The University of Southern Denmark approved a trial that would randomize about fourteen thousand newborns in Guinea-Bissau to receive the hepatitis B vaccine at birth or to follow the national schedule, which starts at six weeks. It is funded by the United States Centers for Disease Control and Prevention. Final authorization from the government of Guinea-Bissau is still pending. The World Health Organization had already questioned it. This is not a result, and the birth-dose recommendation stands.

The American Academy of Pediatrics renewed its call to ban the sale of raw milk and raw dairy products. Nearly half of illnesses from unpasteurized dairy occur in people under twenty. The statement adds a new concern: avian influenza virus, detected in nearly a quarter of raw milk samples from affected areas during the twenty twenty-four dairy outbreak. No human infection from drinking raw milk has been confirmed. The advice extends to unpasteurized artisanal cheeses.

In food allergy, an Australian trial published on September seventeenth in the New England Journal of Medicine came back neutral. Two thousand one hundred thirty-seven pregnant women with a family history of allergy ate more, or less, egg and peanut from mid-pregnancy through four months of breastfeeding. At one year, egg or peanut allergy affected seven point eight percent of infants with high intake and eight point four percent with standard intake. The difference was not significant. A caution: allergy was half as common as expected, so a moderate benefit is not ruled out.

In atopic dermatitis, a United States insurance claims analysis matched two thousand eight hundred ninety-seven children starting dupilumab with the same number receiving systemic corticosteroids. New diagnoses of asthma, allergic rhinitis, or food allergy were thirty-three to fifty-two percent less likely with dupilumab, depending on age. This is a conference poster, observational, not peer reviewed, and staff from the manufacturer took part. It raises a hypothesis; it does not prove the drug halts the atopic march.

Also in atopic dermatitis, the manufacturer Arcutis presented data on October ninth for roflumilast cream, zero point zero five percent, in infants from three months of age. Of ninety-six infants who completed four weeks, thirty-four percent reached clear or almost clear skin. There was no control group, and the source is the company itself.

Two abstracts from the Academy's national conference. The first examined three decades of high school surveys. Suicidal ideation fell until two thousand nine and has risen by more than three and a half percent a year since. It is not peer reviewed and cannot establish causes, but it supports routine screening for depression and suicide risk.

The second reviewed electric bicycle injuries in patients under eighteen. Fractures were the most common injury, at twenty-seven percent, and the head was injured in one in five cases. There were only three hundred five cases; still, a helmet, every time.

In pediatric surgery, a randomized trial at sixteen centers compared caudal and penile block for hypospadias repair in children aged two or younger. Urethrocutaneous fistula occurred in eight point nine percent with caudal block and seven point three percent with penile block, with no significant difference. Caudal block reduced opioid use. It stopped early and appeared on September eleventh, slightly outside our window.

From the Dominican Republic, the bulletin for epidemiological week thirty-eight shows eight confirmed pertussis cases this year, against one last year. Leptospirosis stands at two hundred seventy-nine cases and twenty deaths, nearly double the cases of twenty twenty-five, although no new cases were confirmed that week.

From South Korea, a parliamentary audit found that the drug regulator does not track shortages of children's medicines separately. Of one thousand five hundred thirteen shortage reports since twenty twenty-one, none involved children's fever syrups. What is not measured is not fixed.

On the calendar, Obesity Week runs in Washington from November fourteenth through seventeenth, with detailed results for semaglutide in children aged six to eleven.

Thank you for listening. Until next month.
