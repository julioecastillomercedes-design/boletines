This edition of what's next in pediatrics reviews five decisions by the United States Food and Drug Administration with target dates between late November twenty twenty-six and late March twenty twenty-seven. All of them come from official company announcements, and remember that the agency can act early, late, or say no.

First, Duchenne muscular dystrophy. The decision date for deramiocel, a cardiosphere-derived cell therapy infused intravenously every three months, is November twenty-second, twenty twenty-six. The phase three HOPE three trial, with one hundred six ambulatory and non-ambulatory patients, compared it with placebo, and the company reports a benefit in upper limb function. Today we rely on corticosteroids, givinostat, vamorolone, or exon-skipping drugs for specific mutations, and the cardiomyopathy still lacks a specific therapy. Note that the product received a complete response letter in July twenty twenty-five; the date was extended by three months because the agency classified the twenty-four-month extension data and new analyses as a major amendment, and the company is seeking an indication focused on upper limb function.

Second, developmental and epileptic encephalopathies caused by variants in the S C N two A and S C N eight A genes. A decision on relutrigine, a sodium channel modulator under priority review, is expected on December twenty-seventh, twenty twenty-six, after a three-month extension for additional sensitivity analyses, with no new clinical studies requested. The phase two EMBOLD trial was stopped early for efficacy after an interim analysis. Today these patients usually accumulate several antiseizure drugs without adequate control. According to the report the company filed with the United States securities regulator, the fifty-one patients who received relutrigine had a fifty-three percent placebo-adjusted reduction in seizures over sixteen weeks. The critical point is that this is phase two evidence, reported by the sponsor from a trial stopped early, so the size and durability of the effect should be interpreted with caution.

Third, achondroplasia. Infigratinib, an oral selective inhibitor of fibroblast growth factor receptor three, has a decision date of February fourth, twenty twenty-seven, under priority review. In the double-blind, placebo-controlled phase three PROPEL three trial it increased annualized height velocity by one point seven four centimeters per year over placebo, based on the adjusted mean difference. Today there is no approved oral treatment: the options are injectable C-type natriuretic peptide analogues, daily vosoritide and, in the United States, once-weekly navepegritide, approved in February twenty twenty-six. Remember that height velocity is a surrogate outcome, and we do not yet know the effect on final height or complications.

Fourth, atopic dermatitis in infants. A decision on extending roflumilast cream zero point zero five percent to infants aged three to twenty-four months is expected on February twenty-third, twenty twenty-seven. The data come from an open-label study of one hundred one infants over four weeks, with no new safety signals. Today, under age two, we use emollients, low-potency topical steroids, and crisaborole. Efficacy is exploratory, there was no control group, and long-term exposure has not been studied.

Fifth, respiratory syncytial virus. On March twenty-second, twenty twenty-seven, a decision is expected on clesrovimab for children under two at increased risk during their second season, such as those with congenital heart disease or chronic lung disease. It rests on the SMART trial, which compared it with palivizumab in the first season and gave an open-label dose in the second season to two hundred seventy-six children. Today, the antibody indicated for the second season is nirsevimab. The second season was open-label with no control group: it provides safety and exposure data, which are not equivalent to comparative clinical efficacy and do not tell us what the agency will base its decision on. The European Medicines Agency is also reviewing the application.
