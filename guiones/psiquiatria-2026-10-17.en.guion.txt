Good morning, colleagues. This is the monthly psychiatry bulletin from Boletines Médicos. We cover the most relevant news from September seventeenth to October seventeenth, twenty twenty-six, a month shaped by the European neuropsychopharmacology congress in Munich.

We begin with the regulatory news of the month. On October ninth, the drugmaker Teva announced that the United States Food and Drug Administration had approved Weltruza, a long-acting olanzapine given under the skin once a month, for adults with schizophrenia. In the double-blind phase three Solaris trial, with six hundred seventy-five randomized patients and eight weeks of comparison with placebo, all three doses lowered the positive and negative syndrome scale by nine point eight to eleven point three points more than placebo. According to specialist coverage, there were no cases of post-injection delirium and sedation syndrome in about three thousand five hundred injections. According to the manufacturer, it needs no loading dose, no oral supplementation, and no observation after the injection, although the label keeps a warning about that syndrome. It is a short, company-sponsored trial, and the metabolic profile is still that of olanzapine.

Staying with schizophrenia and metabolism. The Lancet Psychiatry published online on October fourteenth an observational study using electronic health records from the United States, with more than seventy thousand adults living with schizophrenia and type two diabetes. People using glucagon-like peptide one receptor agonists had twenty-four percent fewer psychiatric hospitalizations than those not treated for diabetes. Being observational, it cannot rule out confounding by indication or adherence. Still, it reinforces the priority of treating diabetes well in these patients.

In addiction, a study published on October second in the open-access journal of the American Medical Association followed nearly two hundred thirty thousand young people aged thirteen to twenty-five with opioid use disorder, all covered by Medicaid. About half started treatment and a third engaged in care. Just under half of those engaged received medication, about one in seven of all young people, and among engaged adolescents under eighteen the figure fell below seven percent. Just one in thirty-two stayed on medication for six months. These are administrative data, but the gap is enormous. The problem is not the medicine. It is access and retention.

Turning to perinatal depression. At the European congress, a Copenhagen team presented a randomized trial in one hundred forty-seven pregnant women at high risk of postpartum depression. They received five weeks of training to read and respond to infant emotions, using computer and virtual reality sessions, or usual care. Overall, there was no significant difference. But among women who had given birth before, postpartum depression was five point three percent with training, versus thirty-four point six percent without it. This is a subgroup finding in a small trial not yet peer reviewed, so it only generates hypotheses.

From Sweden comes a finding on suicide prevention. A team from the Karolinska Institute and Umeå University presented in Munich a regional study, from two thousand six to twenty twenty-one, on folic acid dispensing and suicide deaths among people aged twenty to twenty-nine, covering three thousand four hundred sixty-three deaths. They found no consistent protective effect, unlike an American study from twenty twenty-two on suicide attempts. The ecological design does not follow individuals, and the work is not yet peer reviewed. Remember that emergency services and crisis lines are available for anyone who needs them.

In Latin America, Mexico's national statistics institute released its twenty twenty-six self-reported well-being survey. Twenty-one point eight percent of people aged twelve and older report anxiety symptoms, and thirteen point four percent report depression symptoms, with higher figures in women. The suicide mortality rate rose from five point one to six point seven per hundred thousand between twenty fifteen and twenty twenty-five, with the highest rate among people aged fifteen to twenty-nine. These are self-reported symptoms, not diagnoses, but they are a valuable regional benchmark.

In South Korea, an award went to a poster presenting the twenty twenty-six update of the national treatment algorithm for bipolar disorder with mixed features. It is expert consensus, and full publication should be awaited before applying it.

We close with artificial intelligence. Researchers at the London campus of Northeastern University described, in a digital mental health journal, harms linked to using chatbots for companionship or emotional support. They report addiction-like attachment, worsening symptoms, cases involving self-harm, and reinforcement of delusional beliefs, though without frequency data. The practical lesson is to routinely ask patients, especially adolescents, whether they use these tools for their mental health.
