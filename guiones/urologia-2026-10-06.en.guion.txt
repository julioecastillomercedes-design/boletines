Good morning, colleagues. This urology bulletin covers September sixth to October sixth, twenty twenty-six. The month was dominated by urologic oncology and by three new guidelines. We include nine verified items. We found no verifiable items from the period in stones, andrology, pediatric urology or robotic surgery, and none confirmed from Asia, the Middle East or Latin America.

We begin with muscle-invasive bladder cancer. On September eighteenth, the medicines committee of the European Medicines Agency recommended perioperative enfortumab vedotin plus pembrolizumab in resectable disease, regardless of cisplatin eligibility. In the Keynote Bee Fifteen trial, with eight hundred eight patients, the comparator was neoadjuvant gemcitabine and cisplatin. Two-year event-free survival was seventy-nine point four percent versus sixty-six point two percent. The hazard ratio was zero point five three. Pathologic complete response was fifty-five point eight versus thirty-two point five percent. Critical reading: this is the strongest change of standard this year. But it is manufacturer-sponsored, and its cost will limit near-term access in the Dominican public system.

In localized prostate cancer, the Pace A trial was presented at the annual meeting of the American Society for Radiation Oncology. One hundred twenty-three men were randomized to prostatectomy or five-fraction stereotactic body radiotherapy. At a median of eight years, failure-free survival was ninety-one percent with radiotherapy and eighty-four percent with surgery. The difference was not significant. At five years, daily pad use was eight versus forty-eight percent. Critical reading: with only thirteen events, the trial is underpowered for superiority, but it gives long-term randomized data for shared decision-making.

Back to the bladder. On September twenty-fifth, the United States Food and Drug Administration accepted with priority review perioperative durvalumab with neoadjuvant enfortumab vedotin. It targets patients who are ineligible for cisplatin or decline it. Per AstraZeneca, the doublet significantly improved event-free and overall survival in the Volga trial. Critical reading: full data have not been published and the information comes from the sponsor.

The phase two Steel trial added enzalutamide to salvage radiotherapy in one hundred eighty-eight patients with high-risk biochemical recurrence after prostatectomy. Two-year progression-free survival was eighty-eight versus seventy-nine percent. The hazard ratio was zero point six two, with a p value of zero point zero five two. Grade three adverse events were thirty versus nineteen percent. Critical reading: a positive signal, but phase two, with borderline significance and poor tolerability; it is not practice-changing.

The American Society for Radiation Oncology issued its first guideline on radiotherapy for bladder cancer. It recommends trimodality therapy as an alternative to cystectomy in selected patients. Critical reading: it offers a formal framework for bladder preservation in patients without timely access to cystectomy, always within a multidisciplinary tumor board.

The American Urological Association and the Society of Urologic Oncology updated their guideline on non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer. It was published on September third, outside the period. For disease unresponsive to bacillus Calmette-Guérin, it adds the Tar two hundred device, with a complete response of seventy-eight point eight percent at three months. Critical reading: several options depend on costly drugs not registered in the region.

On September thirtieth, the agency granted Fast Track designation to Telix's Bipass program. It combines prostate-specific membrane antigen positron emission tomography with magnetic resonance imaging before biopsy. In the Primary two study, biopsy was avoided in forty-nine percent. Critical reading: this is a designation, not an approval, and lower specificity and cost limit its role as a screening tool in our setting.

The Canadian Urological Association released a national consensus on prostate cancer theranostics, with thirty-two statements from seventeen experts. Critical reading: it is expert consensus rather than a systematic review, but it offers practical guidance for programs starting this imaging.

Finally, in overactive bladder, FemPulse announced a European conformity mark for its non-implanted vaginal neuromodulation ring. Critical reading: it is attractive because it is non-invasive, but current evidence does not justify preferring it over neuromodulation options backed by randomized data.
