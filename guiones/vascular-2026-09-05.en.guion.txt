Good morning, colleagues. Period covered: August twenty-ninth to September fifth, two thousand twenty-six. It was a slow week for hard evidence, with no new randomized trials in peripheral, aortic, carotid or venous disease. What there was, was mostly regulatory and industry news. There are eleven items, no filler, priority areas first.

We start below the knee. The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services approved a new technology add-on payment for the Spur recoverable stent from Reflow Medical. It is a temporary scaffold that is removed without leaving an implant. The incremental payment is up to two thousand five hundred ninety-six dollars and seventy-five cents per inpatient Medicare case, starting October first. Critical reading: there is no randomized comparison against angioplasty or a drug-coated balloon. It changes reimbursement, not evidence.

Abbott obtained the European conformity mark for the seventy-six millimeter version of its Esprit resorbable scaffold, in chronic limb-threatening ischemia. The Food and Drug Administration had approved it in July. Critical reading: the evidence base remains the Life trial, in short lesions. There are no published data for this length or for long lesions.

In prevention, the Australian Staree trial tested forty milligrams of atorvastatin in nine thousand nine hundred seventy-one participants aged seventy or older without cardiovascular disease. Major cardiovascular events were six point zero versus eight point three percent, with a hazard ratio of zero point seven zero. There was no difference in disability-free survival. Critical reading: it excluded diabetics and established cardiovascular disease. It does not apply to already-diagnosed peripheral artery disease.

Data from the Vascular Annual Meeting two thousand twenty-six linked clopidogrel metabolizer gene variants to risk after peripheral intervention. Roughly twenty percent of patients carried the variants. There were no more limb events, but more major cardiovascular events over the following year. Critical reading: the coverage gives no sample size or effect magnitude, and the study is observational.

The Dasbad N C trial was announced, with one hundred forty-two patients. It will compare a directional scoring balloon with a non-compliant balloon in the femoropopliteal segment. Critical reading: it is an announcement only, run in partnership with the manufacturer. It does not change practice.

Shockwave Medical begins operating as Johnson and Johnson MedTech on October first. Critical reading: this week's supplement on intravascular lithotripsy is sponsored content with no new figures.

In the aorta, the Medtronic Endurant stent graft received the European conformity mark to remove the ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm warning from its label. Critical reading: it formalizes standard practice and has medicolegal implications, but there is no new evidence in ruptured aneurysm.

The Food and Drug Administration issued an early alert on the recall of Boston Scientific Imager Two angiographic catheters. There is a risk of tip detachment, with two serious injuries and no deaths. Critical reading: it is a high-volume diagnostic catheter. Check local stock and notify interventional and radiology teams.

In pulmonary embolism, the pivotal Trust study of the Laguna thrombectomy system completed enrollment of one hundred seven patients. Critical reading: it is single-arm, with no randomized comparator. What matters will come at the Viva meeting.

In hemodialysis access, a commentary in Vascular News reviewed the European study of the aXess graft, with one hundred twenty patients. According to that commentary, twelve-month primary patency was thirty-four percent. The manufacturer confirms a cumulative patency of seventy-nine percent. Critical reading: primary patency does not exceed polytetrafluoroethylene, and the comparison is historical.

Finally, the Photonova Spectra photon-counting computed tomography system from General Electric HealthCare received the European conformity mark. Critical reading: according to the manufacturer, spectral imaging may help distinguish iodine, calcium and fat. But there are no published vascular clinical data.

Thank you for listening. Until next week.
