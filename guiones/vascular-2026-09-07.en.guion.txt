Good morning, colleagues. This is the vascular bulletin for the week of August thirty-first to September seventh, two thousand twenty-six. It was a slow week for clinical evidence: the European cardiology congress brought no peripheral vascular trials. Regulatory and industry news dominate, with eight items and figures verified against the source.

We start with Medicare, which approved a new technology add-on payment for the Spur recoverable stent in the infrapopliteal territory. It will pay up to two thousand five hundred ninety-six dollars and seventy-five cents per case, starting October first, two thousand twenty-six. The payment will last a maximum of two years. The basis is Deeper Reveal, with one hundred thirty patients at forty-nine centers and a single arm. At twelve months, in vessels of two point five millimeters or more, primary patency was seventy-eight percent. Freedom from revascularization was eighty-three point one percent. Complete wound healing was eighty-four point three percent in the paired analysis. These are not figures for all patients, and the manufacturer does not report denominators. Critical reading: this is reimbursement, not new evidence, with no comparator and with manufacturer data.

At the two thousand twenty-six Vascular Annual Meeting, a study examined clopidogrel metabolism variants after peripheral intervention. About twenty percent of patients were carriers. They had no increase in limb events, but more major adverse cardiovascular events, including myocardial infarction and stroke, over the following year. Critical reading: it is observational, with no effect-size figures, and does not support routine genotyping.

In hemodialysis access, twelve-month data for Axess, with one hundred twenty patients, showed primary patency of thirty-four percent. Assisted patency was forty-nine percent and cumulative patency seventy-nine percent. Critical reading: these figures are similar to expanded polytetrafluoroethylene, and data from the ongoing American trial are still pending.

In pulmonary embolism, the Trust trial completed enrollment with the Laguna device: one hundred seven patients at seventeen centers. Results will be presented at the Viva meeting on October fifth. Critical reading: it is single-arm, and there are no data yet.

In the aorta, Medtronic received the European conformity mark on September third for the Endurant stent graft without the ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysm warning. This aligns Europe with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration. It relies on the Engage registry at ten years. Critical reading: there are no new rupture figures.

Abbott received the European conformity mark for the seventy-six millimeter Esprit device for below-the-knee use. Critical reading: it is a line extension with no data of its own. The evidence remains the Life Bee Tee Kay trial, with short lesions.

Shockwave becomes part of Johnson and Johnson MedTech as of October first. A supplement on intravascular lithotripsy in calcified chronic limb-threatening ischemia was also published. Critical reading: it is sponsored, with no new numerical data.

Finally, General Electric received the European conformity mark for the Photonova Spectra photon-counting scanner. According to the manufacturer, spectral imaging may help distinguish iodine, calcium and fat. Critical reading: there are no peripheral vascular studies.

As for meetings, the Cardiovascular and Interventional Radiological Society of Europe congress runs in Copenhagen through September ninth. The Viva meeting will be in Las Vegas from October fourth to seventh. The European Society for Vascular Surgery meets in Belgrade from October sixth to ninth.

Always verify the primary source before applying any data. Thank you for listening. Until next week.
