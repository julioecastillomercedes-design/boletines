Good morning, colleagues. This is the weekly vascular bulletin for the week of September seventh to fourteenth, twenty twenty-six. It was a slow week in the priority areas. There were no new trials, major publications or regulatory approvals in calcium, atherectomy, intravascular lithotripsy or drug-coated balloons and stents in the femoropopliteal or infrapopliteal segments. Activity concentrated around the European cardiovascular and interventional radiology congress in Copenhagen. The news came mainly in hemodialysis access and pulmonary embolism. Six verified items are sent rather than padding the issue.

We start with hemodialysis access. Two-year results of the Save trial were presented at that congress. It compared the Selution sirolimus-eluting balloon with high-pressure plain balloon angioplasty in arteriovenous fistula stenosis. It is a randomized, multicenter, single-blind trial. Functional primary lesion patency at six months was seventy-eight percent versus fifty-eight point five percent. The advantage was maintained at two years. The hazard ratio for loss of patency with the plain balloon was two point one four. Its ninety-five percent confidence interval ran from one point one eight to three point nine one. All-cause mortality at two years was seven point one versus nine point five percent. There were no serious device-related adverse events. The critical reading: according to the investigator, it is the first prospective randomized trial in dysfunctional fistulas. But the coverage reports neither the number of patients nor the funding, and no peer-reviewed publication is mentioned.

Also in hemodialysis, the Discover study assessed the Solaris sirolimus-eluting covered stent at six months. It is a phase two feasibility study of one hundred twenty patients. Eighty fistula patients were randomized against plain balloon angioplasty, and forty graft patients were treated in a single arm. Six-month primary lesion patency was ninety-two point zero percent versus sixty-five point nine percent in fistulas. In grafts it was ninety-three point five percent. At thirty days, ninety-seven point three percent were event-free. The critical reading: a strong signal, but still phase two, with short follow-up and a graft arm with no comparator. It comes from a company release, and it still needs comparison with covered stents already on the market.

In pulmonary embolism, the Strike registry presented a one-year functional and quality-of-life subanalysis with the sixteen French Lightning Flash catheter. Of the first one thousand patients with intermediate or high-risk embolism, five hundred eighty-four were treated with that catheter. Two hundred eight have one-year functional follow-up. The right-to-left ventricular ratio fell from one point three six to zero point nine six at forty-eight hours. Major adverse events at forty-eight hours were one point five percent. The six-minute walk distance went from three hundred forty-seven meters at ninety days to three hundred eighty-four meters at one year. The critical reading: there is no anticoagulation-alone control arm, and follow-up covers only a fraction of the cohort. The improvement cannot be attributed to the device.

In the infrapopliteal segment, the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services granted a new technology add-on payment for the Spur retrievable stent from Reflow Medical. The device provides temporary scaffolding in arteries of two point five to four point five millimeters and is removed without leaving an implant. The incremental payment is up to two thousand five hundred ninety-six dollars and seventy-five cents per inpatient Medicare case. It applies from October first, twenty twenty-six, for up to two years. The critical reading: this is a reimbursement story, not an efficacy one. There are no randomized data against plain balloon angioplasty or drug-coated balloons, and the release comes from the manufacturer.

In the aorta, Medtronic's Endurant endograft received the European conformity mark to remove a warning from its instructions for use. It no longer states that it was not evaluated in ruptured abdominal aortic aneurysms. This follows the United States Food and Drug Administration, which made that change in October twenty twenty-five. The critical reading: it formalizes a practice that was already widespread and may facilitate urgent endovascular repair. But it provides no outcome figures in ruptured aneurysms and does not change the clinical indication.

Finally, Penumbra launched Link, a clot-capture device for arterial thrombectomy. It is deployed inside the organized thrombus to engage it while the Lightning Bolt catheter extracts it with computer-assisted vacuum. The report includes only anecdotal experience from a single user. The critical reading: this is a commercial launch with no clinical data and no technical success or distal embolization rates. Independent series should be awaited before adoption.
