Good morning, colleagues. This is the week of September fourteenth to twenty-first, twenty twenty-six. It was slow in the specialty press but active in the journals. There are fourteen items; seven come from outside North America and Western Europe: Japan, Czechia, India, China and Saudi Arabia.

We start with the Czech Prague twenty-six trial, published in the New England Journal of Medicine. Five hundred fifty-eight patients with intermediate-high-risk pulmonary embolism received catheter-directed thrombolysis plus anticoagulation, or anticoagulation alone. Death, recurrence or decompensation at seven days: zero point seven versus six point eight percent. There were two intracranial hemorrhages versus none. It is open-label, with very few events and near-zero mortality. It is included outside the seven-day window because of its weight.

The Best Cli trial registry followed one thousand nine patients with chronic limb-threatening ischemia, including those not eligible for revascularization. At one year, twenty-five point six percent had died, and major amputation was eight point two percent. The rates are clearly worse than in the randomized trial. Mortality, not the limb, dominates prognosis.

The American varicose vein registry of the Vascular Quality Initiative gathered forty-four thousand eight hundred eighty-three patients. Thermal ablation fell from ninety-one point five to fifty-three point zero percent of procedures. Even so, it achieved greater improvement in the venous clinical severity score than non-thermal ablation. It is the benchmark against which any new saphenous occlusion technique will have to be compared.

In Italy, a series of three hundred forty-nine limbs treated great saphenous reflux with distal ligation and catheter-directed foam in the excluded segment. At five years, the excluded segment recanalized in six point three percent. Varicose vein recurrence was twenty-four point seven percent. It is a direct benchmark for endovenous ligation devices, although it has no comparator.

In femoropopliteal claudication, an analysis linked to Medicare data compared seven hundred twenty bypasses with three thousand two hundred fifty-nine endovascular interventions. At five years, major adverse limb events were similar: fifty-seven point zero versus fifty-nine point one percent. The difference was not significant. Matching does not remove indication bias.

In Japan, a subanalysis of two hundred twenty-two femoropopliteal lesions treated with drug-coated balloons used intravascular ultrasound. Circumferential calcification was an independent predictor of loss of patency. Each square millimeter less of minimal lumen area raised restenosis risk by thirty-two percent. The goal of plaque preparation is the lumen area measured by imaging.

Also from Japan, the Joker cohort treated two hundred thirty-eight patients with rotational atherectomy in calcified femoropopliteal lesions. More than a third were on hemodialysis. Primary patency at one year was eighty-three point eight percent. Without a control group, it does not show an incremental benefit of atherectomy.

An Italian meta-analysis of sixty-three studies examined type one A endoleak after endovascular aneurysm repair. Overall technical success was ninety-five point two percent. Fenestrated or branched repair was the most used option. The certainty of the evidence is very low.

From Saudi Arabia, a meta-analysis of forty-five studies reviewed lower-limb hemodialysis access. Pooled primary patency was sixty-two percent at twelve months. Autologous fistula outperformed prosthetic graft. Heterogeneity is very high and all the evidence is observational.

In China, a single-center study compared two thrombectomy systems in two hundred forty-three patients with acute iliofemoral deep vein thrombosis. At thirty-six months, post-thrombotic syndrome was more frequent with the rheolytic system. It was thirty-four point nine five versus twenty-one point six seven percent. Allocation was not random.

In the United States, a series of twenty-two patients treated post-thrombotic obstruction in refractory venous ulcers with mechanical thrombectomy. Seventy-six percent of the ulcers closed completely. It is small, uncontrolled and with very short follow-up.

Another Chinese cohort of five hundred patients added a venoactive drug after great saphenous radiofrequency ablation. Complications were three point six versus twelve point eight percent. It is retrospective, unblinded, and the drug is not specified.

In India, a trial of sixty-six patients with venous ulcer compared a dressing alone, the dressing with a compression bandage, and a four-layer system. All three products come from the same manufacturer. Overall complete closure was forty-two point six percent. With twenty patients per arm, it is a hypothesis, not evidence.

Finally, in Italy, twenty-four patients with chronic limb-threatening ischemia received a sirolimus balloon in femoropopliteal lesions. At thirty months, primary patency was ninety-five point five percent. With so few patients, nothing can be concluded about safety or efficacy.
