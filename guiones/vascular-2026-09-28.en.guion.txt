Good morning, colleagues. This bulletin covers the week of September twenty-first to twenty-eighth, twenty twenty-six. It was a week of moderate volume. The trade press had little news because the major fall meetings take place next week. Searches in Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Russian, Arabic, Turkish and Portuguese found no verifiable publications dated this week.

The heaviest item is the three-paper clinical Series on peripheral artery disease published by The Lancet on September twenty-second, open access. According to the American College of Cardiology summary, more than one hundred thirteen million people aged forty and older have the disease. The abstract of the third paper cites more than three hundred million affected. When diagnosed at an advanced stage, five-year mortality is between thirty and fifty percent. The other two papers cover medical, endovascular and surgical treatment, and disparities and global burden. Critical reading: it is a narrative review and generates no new evidence. But it sets the epidemiological frame that guidelines and payers will cite, and it reinforces ankle-brachial index detection in risk groups.

In Europe, a debate opened over paclitaxel in claudication. The European Society for Vascular Surgery published a focused guideline update, driven mainly by the Swedepad two trial. That trial randomized one thousand one hundred fifty-five claudicants. Paclitaxel-coated devices did not improve quality of life or reintervention at twelve months versus uncoated devices. There was also a signal of excess mortality at five years. The guideline puts supervised exercise and best medical therapy first. It reserves endovascular revascularization for persistent lifestyle-limiting symptoms. Paclitaxel devices may be considered only for selected cases of restenosis. Five interventionalists replied that the guideline rests on a single trial from a single country. They note forty point four percent Rutherford category one and two patients, a subjective primary endpoint, and no core lab. They cite a nationwide cohort of two hundred fifty-nine thousand one hundred thirty-seven adults with no association between paclitaxel and mortality. Critical reading: four of the five comment authors report manufacturer ties, but their methodological objections are legitimate. For those treating claudicants, the practical message is to document failure of conservative therapy before intervening.

On the venous side, the British Medical Journal published a Danish nested case-control study in women aged fifty to sixty-nine. It included nine thousand eight hundred seven venous thromboembolism cases versus forty-nine thousand thirty-five controls. Oral hormone therapy was associated with higher risk regardless of dose and duration. The absolute excess was zero point zero nine percent per year. That is one additional case per one thousand fifty-five women treated per year. Stroke and infarction increased only with oral estradiol above one milligram a day for more than one year. Transdermal therapy was generally not associated with excess thrombosis. One exception rested on sparse data: more infarction with combined cyclic transdermal therapy, hazard ratio two point one. Critical reading: it is observational, without smoking or body mass index data. In the venous clinic, for patients with prior thrombosis or high risk, transdermal is the reasonable option.

Venous News published an interview on the European Venous Registry of deep venous disease. It has more than fifty sites, more than five hundred fifty entries and ethics approval in twenty countries. The early insights are qualitative. They show wide variation in anesthesia, adjuncts and above all intravascular ultrasound use. A meaningful share of patients had no good outcome at three months, under conservative or interventional care. Critical reading: there are no outcome figures yet. But it is the first European initiative that can generate industry-independent performance goals for venous stenting and thrombectomy.

The German Society for Vascular Surgery held its forty-second annual congress in Frankfurt. It called for targeted ankle-brachial index screening in risk groups. It cited Hamburg data in which nearly a quarter of participants aged forty-five to seventy-four had an index below zero point nine. It also presented a position paper with geriatrics and angiology on frailty assessment. Critical reading: these are society positions, not new data. But frailty should be kept in mind in elderly patients with aneurysm or chronic limb-threatening ischemia.

In vascular access, Dialybrid began a first-in-human trial in Italy of its silk and polyurethane arteriovenous graft. It will enroll eighteen adults at four centers, with planned follow-up of sixty months. Critical reading: it is a press release without results. Twelve-month patency will have to be seen before drawing conclusions.

Looking ahead, The Veins takes place in Las Vegas on October third and fourth, and Viva on October fourth to seventh. The European Society for Vascular Surgery annual meeting will be in Belgrade, October sixth to ninth.

Thank you for listening. Until next week.
