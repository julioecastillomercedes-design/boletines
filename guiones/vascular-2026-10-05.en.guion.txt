Good morning, colleagues. This summary covers September twenty-eighth to October fifth, twenty twenty-six. The week was dominated by venous disease, with eight late-breaking studies at The Veins meeting in Las Vegas. We gathered ten verified items.

The randomized C Tract trial presented its twenty-four-month follow-up. It enrolled two hundred twenty-five patients with post-thrombotic syndrome and iliac vein obstruction. It compared iliac vein stenting plus standard care with standard care alone. The Veines quality of life score was sixty-three point five versus fifty-three point zero. Recurrent symptomatic venous thromboembolism was two point one versus seven point one percent. Major bleeding was four point one versus two point zero percent, with no difference between groups according to the source. Critical reading: this is the best randomized evidence for stenting, and it is independent of industry. But the trial was open label, the outcome is subjective, and quality of life was analyzed in only one hundred ninety-seven patients.

The single-arm Bolt study treated two hundred twenty-nine patients with acute iliofemoral deep vein thrombosis using computer-assisted vacuum thrombectomy. Thrombus reduction of at least seventy-five percent was achieved in ninety-eight point zero percent. At thirty days, re-thrombosis was three point nine percent. Critical reading: it is manufacturer-sponsored with no comparator. It shows the device removes thrombus with acceptable safety, not that it reduces post-thrombotic syndrome compared with anticoagulation alone.

In active venous ulcers, the Spectrum study followed one hundred twenty-five patients treated with VenaSeal cyanoacrylate from Medtronic. At twenty-four months, ulcer healing was eighty-eight point one percent and recurrence twenty-two point nine percent. The probability of anatomic vein closure was seventy-eight point zero percent. Critical reading: there is no comparator and the study belongs to the manufacturer. Anatomic closure of seventy-eight percent at two years deserves scrutiny.

According to trade press coverage, the United States Food and Drug Administration cleared Venice, a percutaneous clip for ligating superficial and perforator veins. The clip is deployed under ultrasound to compress and occlude the vein, with no heat, no tumescence and no adhesive. In an interim analysis of twenty-two patients, technical success was one hundred percent. At one month, all twenty-two great saphenous veins and ten of eleven perforators remained closed. Six-month data exist for only eight saphenous veins and three perforators, all closed. Critical reading: it is a single center, with adjunctive sclerotherapy confounding the effect of the clip. The primary endpoint, closure at twelve months, is not yet available, and this clearance pathway does not require comparative efficacy.

The real-world registry of the Abre venous stent from Medtronic enrolled one hundred fifty-one patients. At thirty days there were no fractures and a single target lesion revascularization. Critical reading: thirty days say nothing about durability. What matters is that three of four implants were for nonthrombotic lesions, the indication with the weakest evidence.

In pulmonary embolism, the Strike study treated one thousand patients with vacuum thrombectomy, with five hundred twenty-two analyzed at one year. The right-to-left ventricular ratio fell from one point three eight to zero point nine seven at forty-eight hours. The Rapid study, using mechanically channeled thrombolysis, reported death or decompensation at seven days in one of one hundred fifty patients. Critical reading: these are manufacturer-sponsored single-arm series with surrogate endpoints. Only randomized trials can answer the clinical question.

In peripheral arterial disease, Medtronic announced a breakthrough device designation for its In Pact below-the-knee paclitaxel balloon. It plans a randomized trial of about four hundred patients. Critical reading: the designation is not evidence of efficacy. The company's earlier balloon showed no benefit and was withdrawn more than a decade ago.

In China, the national medical products regulator admitted an absorbable covered peripheral stent from Yuanxin Technology to its special review pathway. Critical reading: entering the priority pathway is not an approval, and no clinical data have been published. It signals that absorbable iron scaffolds are moving into the peripheral territory.

In carotid disease, the Journal of the American Medical Association published in September the cognitive results of the Crest two trials, with more than two thousand one hundred participants. In severe asymptomatic stenosis, revascularization did not improve learning, attention, memory or executive function compared with medical therapy alone, with results maintained at four years. Critical reading: it removes a common argument for intervening; the indication should be discussed only in terms of stroke prevention.

Finally, Gore started the pivotal study of its Excluder iliac branch endoprosthesis for aortoiliac aneurysms, at about thirty United States sites. Critical reading: this is the start of enrollment, with no clinical data, and it does not change practice.
