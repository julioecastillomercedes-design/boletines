Good morning, colleagues. This is the weekly vascular bulletin for October twelfth, twenty twenty-six. We cover October fifth through twelfth, the week of the Viva meeting in Las Vegas. Nearly all of the arterial news comes from meeting presentations, mostly unpublished and released by the manufacturers. Please hear them with that caution in mind.

We begin with sirolimus-coated balloons in the femoropopliteal segment. The Selution Four S F A trial randomized three hundred patients with symptomatic peripheral artery disease. The primary efficacy endpoint at one year was seventy-eight point six percent with the sirolimus balloon, versus sixty-five point two percent with a plain balloon. A secondary analysis of the Sir Pad trial, in four hundred twenty-one patients without tissue loss, showed target lesion revascularization of seventeen percent versus twenty-seven point one percent. The critical reading: the comparator is still a plain balloon, not paclitaxel, and follow-up is one year.

Next, below the knee. The postapproval study of the Esprit bioresorbable scaffold enrolled two hundred patients with no comparison group. At six months, amputation-free survival was eighty-eight percent. In the randomized Motiv trial, with two hundred ninety-two patients, the benefit over plain angioplasty held in patients with tissue loss: at twelve months, sixty-five point seven percent versus forty-six point two percent. This is a subgroup analysis released by the manufacturer, and the device remains investigational.

Third, intravascular lithotripsy below the knee at two years. The Disrupt P A D B T K Two study treated two hundred forty-three patients with the Shockwave system, most with chronic limb-threatening ischemia. At twenty-four months, ninety-four point eight percent remained free from major amputation. But mortality was nearly twenty-four percent, there is no comparison group, and many patients were lost to follow-up.

A second lithotripsy system is arriving. The Power P A D Two study enrolled one hundred fifteen patients with calcified femoral and popliteal arteries. Patency was ninety-one percent at six months. It is a single arm, with no head-to-head comparison with the established system.

For patients with no revascularization option, the Promise Three study evaluated transcatheter arterialization of the deep veins in one hundred patients. At one year, limb salvage was eighty-four point one percent. There is still no control group.

In the iliac arteries, the Agility study evaluated the Revello self-expanding covered stent in one hundred twenty patients. At nine months, primary patency was ninety-seven point two percent, measured against a performance goal rather than against balloon-expandable covered stents.

From Japan, Annals of Vascular Diseases published a series of eighty-nine saphenous vein bypasses on October seventh. A thicker medial layer of the vein, seen in limbs with worse wounds, was associated with loss of patency. It is a small series, but a reminder that diameter is not everything.

Turning to pulmonary embolism. The Trust study evaluated the Laguna thrombectomy system in one hundred seven intermediate-risk patients. The right-to-left ventricular ratio improved at forty-eight hours, with a single major adverse event. This is a single-arm manufacturer study with a surrogate endpoint.

A post hoc analysis of the randomized High Peitho trial examined bleeding with ultrasound-facilitated catheter thrombolysis. Major bleeding was four percent, versus three percent with anticoagulation alone, with no significant difference, and there were no intracranial hemorrhages. The bleeding predictors are exploratory.

In the Storm P E trial, a smartwatch substudy found, in thirty-three patients, a moderate correlation between daily steps and the six-minute walk test at one month. It is a correlation, not a treatment effect.

Two regulatory decisions. The United States Food and Drug Administration expanded the indication of the Lobo vascular occluder, from Okami Medical, to the peripheral veins, with no new clinical data. And in Brazil, the health agency Anvisa authorized the Impede embolization plugs, from Shape Memory Medical.

In carotid disease, the Performance Two study reported five minor strokes through one year among three hundred five patients treated with a stent with an integrated filter. And in industry news, B. Braun will close its interventional division in the United States, which includes vena cava filters, and will continue sales through March thirty-first, twenty twenty-seven.

On the calendar, the Site symposium in Santiago de Compostela runs from October fourteenth to sixteenth, and the T C T meeting in San Diego runs from October thirty-first to November third.
