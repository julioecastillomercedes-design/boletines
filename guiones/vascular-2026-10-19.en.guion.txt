Good morning, colleagues. This is the vascular news bulletin for October nineteenth, two thousand twenty-six. We cover the week of October thirteenth to nineteenth. Most of it comes from the Viva and Veins meetings in Las Vegas, plus two Food and Drug Administration authorizations announced by their manufacturers. Ten stories. We begin with venous disease.

The lead story is Bolt D V T, the pivotal trial of computer-assisted vacuum thrombectomy with the Indigo system, from Penumbra, in acute iliofemoral deep vein thrombosis. Two hundred twenty-nine patients at thirty-nine sites in the United States. Both primary endpoints were met: in ninety-eight percent of evaluable patients the thrombus was reduced by at least seventy-five percent, and the forty-eight hour major adverse event rate was three point one percent, with no device-related deaths. Critical reading: single-arm, sponsored by the manufacturer, anatomic endpoint, short follow-up. Whether it prevents post-thrombotic syndrome compared with anticoagulation alone remains without a randomized answer.

InterVene presented at Veins a prospective study of its Recana thrombectomy system in venous in-stent restenosis and post-thrombotic obstruction. Patency was achieved in ninety-six percent of limbs, and lumen area grew roughly sevenfold. Critical reading: the endpoint is anatomic and immediate, and neither the number of patients nor the complications were reported.

Medtronic presented the first data from its registry of the Abre venous stent: one hundred fifty-one patients with iliofemoral venous obstruction at fifteen sites in Europe and the United States. At thirty days there were no stent fractures and a single repeat revascularization. Critical reading: at one month a registry can only speak to technical safety; patency at one and five years is still to come.

In intermediate-risk pulmonary embolism, the Rapid P E study from Thrombolex presented an interim analysis of its first one hundred fifty patients, out of three hundred fifty planned. Technical success was one hundred percent, death or hemodynamic decompensation at seven days zero point seven percent, and major bleeding zero point seven percent. Ninety percent needed no intensive care. Critical reading: an interim analysis with no comparator, sponsored by the manufacturer, and without ventricular ratio data yet.

Also in pulmonary embolism, the Food and Drug Administration cleared the Helo thrombectomy system and the Helo Return blood return system, from Endovascular Engineering, which filters aspirated blood and gives it back to the patient. The company reports near-zero blood loss in the Engulf study. Critical reading: the clearance was announced on October twelfth, one day before our period, and the press release gives no figures for clot reduction or adverse events.

An eye-catching but still barely verifiable story comes from Romania. The company Veinsound began there its first-in-human study of artificial-intelligence guided focused ultrasound to occlude the great saphenous vein in the office, without heat, needles, catheters or anesthesia. Four patients were treated in under twelve minutes, with occlusion observed at one week. Critical reading: so far there are only company and investigator statements, with no trial registration and no defined occlusion criteria.

On the arterial side, the Elegance registry presented at Viva twelve-month results in three hundred eighty-two patients treated with the Eluvia paclitaxel-eluting stent, from Boston Scientific, in complex femoropopliteal lesions. Primary patency was ninety-three percent and freedom from major amputation ninety-nine percent. Critical reading: single-arm, manufacturer-sponsored, no comparator.

Also at Viva, a session on peripheral artery disease noted that only about half of patients receive full guideline-directed medical therapy after an intervention. Critical reading: this is not a study but a session with observational data.

In aortic disease, the Food and Drug Administration approved on October fourteenth the Viabahn V B X endoprosthesis, from Gore, as a bridging stent in fenestrated and branched endovascular repair. It rests on three-year results of the Embrace registry: primary patency of ninety-seven point five percent in fenestrated and ninety-four percent in branched repair. Critical reading: according to Gore, this is the first bridging stent with this dual on-label indication in the United States, but the registry is single-arm and run by the manufacturer.

And in the Journal of Vascular Surgery, an analysis of more than sixty-two thousand patients repaired for abdominal aortic aneurysm. Rural patients had more urgent presentations, more loss to follow-up and, among men, larger aneurysms. Critical reading: observational and zip-code based.

November fourth to seventh, the Silan congress in Guadalajara. And November seventeenth to twenty-first, the Veith symposium in New York.
