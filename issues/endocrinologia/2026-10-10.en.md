This issue covers what was published, presented or approved between September 28 and October 10, 2026. It was dominated by the second half of the EASD meeting in Milan, with four simultaneous publications in NEJM, The Lancet and The Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology on drugs for diabetes and obesity; there is also a Korean approval of a domestically developed GLP-1 and an FDA approval in thyroid disease. Nine items; one from outside North America and Western Europe (Korea). Bone and reproductive endocrinology have no items in this issue.

## Type 1 and type 2 diabetes

### 1. Survodutide (dual glucagon/GLP-1 agonist) in obesity with type 2 diabetes: 9.8% weight loss at 76 weeks (SYNCHRONIZE-2)
[Clinical trial] [International / Germany]
Published in NEJM on October 1 and presented at EASD: phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (NCT06066528), 755 randomized adults (752 treated) with obesity or overweight and type 2 diabetes (baseline HbA1c 7.4%), weekly survodutide 3.6 or 6.0 mg for 76 weeks. With the treatment-regimen estimand, weight loss was 8.2% and 9.8% vs 3.9% with placebo; with the efficacy estimand, up to 13.1% (6.0 mg) vs 3.1%. At least 5% weight loss was achieved by 57.6% and 64.5% vs 35.1% with placebo. HbA1c fell 0.9 and 0.8 points vs 0.2 with placebo. Discontinuation due to gastrointestinal events: 18% vs 1.2%; due to any adverse event, 26% vs 8.8%.
Why it matters: with the treatment-regimen analysis, the advantage over placebo is 4.3 to 5.9 points of weight and 0.6 to 0.7 points of HbA1c, and one in four participants discontinued because of adverse events. The 9.8% figure, not the 13.1%, is the one to quote. Funded by Boehringer Ingelheim; no active comparator.
https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2607219
https://www.cardioteca.com/blog-diabetes/8746-estudio-synchronize-2-survodutida-obesidad-diabetes-tipo-2.html
https://www.ajmc.com/view/dual-agonist-survodutide-delivers-up-to-13-1-weight-loss-in-t2d

### 2. Oral orforglipron noninferior to glargine for cardiovascular events (ACHIEVE-4)
[Clinical trial] [International / United States]
Published in The Lancet on September 30 and presented at EASD: phase 3, randomized, open-label, event-driven, noninferiority trial (margin 1.8; NCT05803421), 2,749 adults with type 2 diabetes, BMI ≥25 and established cardiovascular disease or chronic kidney disease, daily orforglipron vs titrated insulin glargine. 4-point MACE: 4.2% vs 5.0%; HR 0.84 (95% CI 0.59-1.20), noninferiority met; 3-point MACE: HR 0.77 (95% CI 0.52-1.13), superiority not shown. At 52 weeks, according to Lilly's press release, HbA1c −1.6 vs −1.0 points (difference −0.66) and weight −8.8% vs +1.7%. All-cause mortality: HR 0.43 (95% CI 0.25-0.75), prespecified analysis not controlled for multiplicity. According to the company, 10.6% of orforglipron recipients discontinued because of adverse events.
Why it matters: this is a safety trial, not a benefit trial; the 1.8 margin is wide, the design open-label, and the comparator (insulin) favors the drug on weight. The mortality signal is exploratory. Funded by Lilly; orforglipron (Foundayo) is already approved in the United States for obesity.
https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/orforglipron-shows-cv-safety-vs-insulin-glargine-in-achieve-4
https://investor.lilly.com/node/54996/pdf

### 3. Eloralintide + tirzepatide: up to 23.3% weight loss in type 2 diabetes (phase 2b)
[Congress] [United States / Argentina]
Presented at EASD and announced by Lilly on September 30 (Billings et al.): 48-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial (NCT06603571), 367 adults with obesity or overweight and type 2 diabetes (baseline weight 105.4 kg; HbA1c 8.1%), enrolled in the United States and Argentina. With the combination of the amylin agonist eloralintide and tirzepatide, weight loss ranged from 13.2% to 23.3% by dose (efficacy estimand), vs 14.8% with tirzepatide 15 mg and 3.0% with placebo; HbA1c −2.2 to −2.9 points (tirzepatide −2.4). Discontinuation due to adverse events 10.8% to 27.0% with the combination vs 2.9% with tirzepatide.
Why it matters: the primary endpoint was versus placebo only; the comparison with tirzepatide is secondary and tolerability clearly worsens at high doses. Company data, no peer-reviewed publication; phase 3 planned for late 2026.
https://www.hcplive.com/view/easd-2026-eloratzp-bests-tirzepatide-on-weight-loss-a1c-in-type-2-diabetes
https://investor.lilly.com/node/54991

### 4. Responding to smart pen alerts is associated with more time in range in type 1 diabetes
[Congress] [United States / International]
Presented at EASD (MiniMed press release of October 5): a six-month randomized trial in 143 people with type 1 diabetes and HbA1c above 8% compared the Smart MDI system (smart pen connected to continuous glucose monitoring) with multiple daily injections plus continuous monitoring and met its noninferiority objective. Within the trial, those who responded more often to missed-dose and high-glucose alerts had 8.1 percentage points more time in range and 0.6 points lower HbA1c than those who responded less. According to the release, a real-world analysis of more than 5,500 users showed a 9.4-point difference in time in range.
Why it matters: the analysis by degree of response is not a randomized comparison, so it confounds adherence with the effect of the technology; the practical message (teach patients to respond to alerts) is reasonable for those already using smart pens with monitoring. Presented by the manufacturer.
https://www.webull.com/news/15695309764903936

### 5. Remission of early-onset type 2 diabetes with a low-energy diet and supervised exercise (RESET for Remission)
[Clinical trial] [Canada / England]
Published in The Lancet Regional Health – Americas (Dasgupta et al., McGill; coverage of October 7): randomized, open-label trial with blinded outcome assessment, at three centers in Canada and England, 96 adults aged 18 to 45 with early-onset type 2 diabetes and obesity, not on insulin. The intervention (800-900 kcal/day for 12 weeks with supervised aerobic and resistance exercise twice weekly, then maintenance to week 24) achieved remission (HbA1c <6.5% off medication for at least 12 weeks) in 54% (27/50) vs 4% (2/46) with usual care.
Why it matters: the effect is large and the intervention does not depend on expensive drugs, which makes it transferable to our setting; but the trial is small, lasted 24 weeks, and we do not know how long remission lasts.
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.lana.2026.101664
https://medicaldialogues.in/diabetes-endocrinology/news/low-energy-diet-plus-supervised-exercise-tied-to-early-onset-type-2-diabetes-remission-lancet-180698

## Obesity and metabolism

### 6. Retatrutide in obesity with type 2 diabetes: full publication of TRIUMPH-2
[Clinical trial] [International / United States]
The Lancet published online on September 29 (presented at EASD) the TRIUMPH-2 trial (NCT05929079): phase 3, double-blind, 80 weeks, 1,152 adults with obesity or overweight and type 2 diabetes (baseline BMI 38.2; HbA1c 7.71%) at 92 sites in 8 countries. With the treatment-regimen estimand, weight loss was 11.9%, 16.8% and 18.8% with 4, 9 and 12 mg, vs 5.1% with placebo (20.8% with 12 mg vs 4.0% with the efficacy estimand in Lilly's press release). HbA1c −1.4 to −1.6 points vs −0.2. With 12 mg vs placebo: nausea 28% vs 8%, diarrhea 34% vs 13%, hypotension 6% vs less than 1% and dysesthesia 7% vs 1%; discontinuation due to adverse events 3.8% to 11.6% by dose vs 4.9%.
Why it matters: the 18.8% figure (treatment regimen), not the 20.8%, is the one to quote. Dysesthesia and hypotension are signals to watch. Funded by Lilly; FDA submission planned for the first quarter of 2027.
https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/retatrutide-cuts-weight-up-to-18-8-in-adults-with-t2d-obesity
https://investor.lilly.com/node/54981/pdf

### 7. Korea approves efpeglenatide, its first domestically developed GLP-1 for obesity
[Regulatory MFDS] [South Korea]
According to the Korean pharmaceutical press, Korea's Ministry of Food and Drug Safety (MFDS) approved on October 7 efpeglenatide (weekly autoinjector, Hanmi Pharmaceutical; 2, 4, 6, 8 and 10 mg) for chronic weight management in adults with obesity or overweight without diabetes, as an adjunct to diet and exercise. It is supported by a phase 3 trial in 448 Korean adults: mean weight loss of 9.75% at 40 weeks (interim result) and about 80% of participants losing at least 5%; adverse events mainly gastrointestinal. The application was filed in December 2025.
Why it matters: efficacy is modest compared with semaglutide or tirzepatide, the data are interim and from an exclusively Korean population, and the press does not detail the placebo group; its relevance for the region is that of one more competitor that could put pressure on prices.
https://dailypharm.com/user/news/343190
https://news.mtn.co.kr/news-detail/2026100717235113087
https://www.medigatenews.com/news/862863720

### 8. Petrelintide, a weekly amylin agonist: up to 10.7% weight loss with good tolerability (ZUPREME-1)
[Publication] [Poland / Romania / United States]
The Lancet Diabetes & Endocrinology published online on September 29 (presented at EASD) the phase 2 ZUPREME-1 trial (NCT06662539): 5:1 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 493 adults with obesity (or overweight with comorbidity) without diabetes at 32 sites, five maintenance doses (1.0 to 9.0 mg) plus dietary counseling. At week 28 (primary endpoint), weight loss of 7.9% to 9.8% vs 1.7% with placebo; at week 42 (exploratory), 8.7% to 10.7%. Nausea 20% vs 6% (80% mild); only 1% discontinued because of gastrointestinal events.
Why it matters: the advantage is tolerability, not magnitude, which falls short of the incretin agonists; its likely place is in combination or in patients who do not tolerate GLP-1 drugs. Phase 2, developed by Zealand Pharma.
https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/petrelintide-yields-up-to-10-7-weight-loss-with-mild-gi-effects

## Thyroid, adrenal and pituitary

### 9. FDA approves tiratricol (Emcitate), first treatment for MCT8 deficiency
[Regulatory FDA] [United States / Sweden]
On September 28 the FDA approved tiratricol (Egetis Therapeutics), a thyroid hormone receptor agonist in tablets dispersed in water, for peripheral thyrotoxicosis in MCT8 transporter deficiency (Allan-Herndon-Dudley syndrome), in adults and children. Approval was based on the phase 3 ReTRIACt trial (NCT05579327), a randomized withdrawal study (15 patients randomized to continue tiratricol or switch to placebo for 30 days), and a 12-month open-label single-arm study in 46 patients: total T3 fell from 323.4 to 118.3 ng/dL, heart rate by 8.9 beats per minute and systolic pressure by 4.1 mm Hg. Warning: the drug interferes with T3 immunoassays and produces falsely elevated values (measurement by mass spectrometry is recommended); it is not recommended for primary hypothyroidism.
Why it matters: it is an ultra-rare X-linked disease, but the interference with T3 measurement is a practical point for any laboratory. The evidence is small: in ReTRIACt the clinical rescue criterion did not show a significant difference, and the demonstrated benefit is on peripheral thyrotoxicosis.
https://www.placera.se/pressmeddelanden/egetis-therapeutics-egetis-therapeutics-announces-u-s-fda-approval-of-emcitate-tiratricol-for-patients-with-mct8-deficiency-20260928
https://www.pharmacytimes.com/view/fda-approves-tiratricol-first-treatment-for-mct8-deficiency
