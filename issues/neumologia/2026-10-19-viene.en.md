This edition covers three pending regulatory decisions in the United States between January and late April 2027: amikacin liposome inhalation suspension in non-refractory MAC lung disease, tozorakimab in COPD, and nebulized treprostinil in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis. Dates come from official company releases; two of them are manufacturer estimates and are labeled as such.

### 1. Amikacin liposome inhalation suspension (ARIKAYCE) for MAC lung disease from diagnosis
[Pending regulatory decision] [United States] [Confirmed date: January 28, 2027 (PDUFA target date)]
What it is: Insmed sNDA for full approval in Mycobacterium avium complex lung disease; the FDA granted priority review (announced September 21, 2026). It is based on ENCORE (phase 3b, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled; n = 425 adults with new or recurrent, previously untreated infection; ARIKAYCE or placebo plus azithromycin and ethambutol for 12 months; primary endpoint: change in respiratory symptom score at month 13; secondary: culture conversion). The company reports the primary and secondary endpoints were met.
What problem it solves: In the US the label is currently limited to refractory MAC (no culture conversion after ≥6 months of multidrug therapy), under accelerated approval; most patients receive the oral regimen alone, with frequent relapses.
Critical take: Insmed-sponsored trial; the release gives no effect sizes (no symptom scores or conversion rates), and cost and tolerability (dysphonia, cough, bronchospasm) will shape use in our setting.

### 2. Tozorakimab (anti-IL-33) in COPD with exacerbations
[Pending regulatory decision] [United States, European Union, China] [Estimated date: first quarter of 2027, per AstraZeneca]
What it is: BLA accepted by the FDA under priority review as add-on maintenance treatment for adults with COPD (announced September 8, 2026); also under review in the EU and China. In OBERON and TITANIA (n = 2,306 randomized; 300 mg every four weeks versus placebo) it reduced moderate/severe exacerbations by 29% and 34% in former smokers (primary endpoint) and by 30% and 29% in the overall population; in the pooled analysis, by 23% with eosinophils <150/µL, 34% with ≥150/µL and 43% with ≥300/µL. Published in NEJM.
What problem it solves: Available COPD biologics (dupilumab, mepolizumab) require an eosinophilic phenotype; many patients who keep exacerbating on triple inhaled therapy have no biologic option.
Critical take: Moderate relative reductions, smaller with low eosinophils; manufacturer-funded trials and a release without confidence intervals. A third phase 3 trial, MIRANDA (n = 1,454), also met its primary endpoint according to AstraZeneca, but its release gave no figures. Price will likely limit access in our region.

### 3. Nebulized treprostinil (Tyvaso) in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis
[Pending regulatory decision] [United States] [Estimated date: late April 2027, per United Therapeutics]
What it is: sNDA accepted by the FDA (acceptance announced by United Therapeutics on September 2, 2026), based on TETON-1 and TETON-2 (phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 52 weeks; primary endpoint: absolute change in FVC). In TETON-2 (n = 597, 75% on background antifibrotics) the difference versus placebo was 95.6 mL (95% CI 52.2–139.0); in the combined analysis of both trials, 111.8 mL (95% CI 79.7–144.0).
What problem it solves: Nintedanib, pirfenidone and nerandomilast slow but do not stop loss of lung function; adding a drug with a different mechanism on top of background therapy is the unmet need.
Critical take: FVC is a surrogate endpoint, there are no survival data, and nebulizing several times a day is a real burden for patients.
