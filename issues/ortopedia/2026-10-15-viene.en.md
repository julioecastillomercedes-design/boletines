This issue covers five milestones with official dates between November 2026 and late April 2027: two pending FDA decisions (an oral drug for achondroplasia and the first amniotic tissue biologic filed for knee osteoarthritis pain) and three specialty meetings. The knee osteoarthritis decision falls a few days past our usual six-month window; we include it because of its weight in everyday practice. PDUFA dates are review target dates, not approvals: the agency may extend the review or issue a complete response letter. We also reviewed phase 3 trials in discogenic low back pain and hip fracture, but their results either fall outside this window or have no public date, so they are not included.

### 1. AAHKS 2026 Annual Meeting (hip and knee)
[Congress] [USA] [Confirmed date: November 5, 2026]
What it is: Annual meeting of the American Association of Hip and Knee Surgeons (AAHKS), November 5–8, 2026, in the Dallas area (Texas), the reference forum for primary and revision arthroplasty.
What problem it solves: Each year it gathers new data on periprosthetic infection, outpatient surgery, analgesia, thromboprophylaxis and technology (robotics, navigation), areas where practice still varies widely between centers.
Critical take: Much of what is presented comes from retrospective or single-center series, often with industry involvement; wait for peer-reviewed publication before changing protocols.
https://meeting.aahks.org/

### 2. Oral infigratinib for children with achondroplasia
[Pending regulatory decision] [USA] [Confirmed date: February 4, 2027]
What it is: A once-daily oral FGFR1-3 inhibitor versus placebo in children with achondroplasia (PROPEL 3, phase 3, NCT06164951); primary endpoint: change in annualized height velocity at 52 weeks, with a least-squares mean difference of +1.74 cm/year (p<0.0001) and improved height Z-score. The FDA accepted the application with priority review (announced by BridgeBio on October 6, 2026); an EMA filing is planned for Q4 2026.
What problem it solves: Currently approved therapies (C-type natriuretic peptide analogues: daily vosoritide and weekly navepegritide) are subcutaneous injections; an oral option would reduce treatment burden over years of growth.
Critical take: Company sponsored, with a surrogate endpoint (growth velocity); there are no data yet on final adult height, fewer spine or limb surgeries, or foramen magnum compression, and cost and access in our setting will be a barrier.
https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/06/3375352/0/en/bridgebio-announces-fda-acceptance-and-priority-review-of-nda-for-oral-infigratinib-for-children-with-achondroplasia.html
https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06164951

### 3. AAOS 2027 Annual Meeting
[Congress] [USA] [Confirmed date: March 3, 2027]
What it is: Annual meeting of the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons, March 3–6, 2027, at the Venetian Convention & Expo Center in Las Vegas; Saturday the 6th is devoted to subspecialty society programs.
What problem it solves: It is the specialty's main annual showcase and the forum where the AAOS usually presents and disseminates its clinical practice guidelines and appropriate use criteria.
Critical take: The volume of abstracts is huge and uneven in quality; what is useful for our practice is usually what is later published in full, not the meeting press release.
https://www.aaos.org/annual/attend/schedule/

### 4. World Congress on Osteoporosis, Osteoarthritis and Musculoskeletal Diseases (WCO-IOF-ESCEO 2027)
[Congress] [Europe (Austria)] [Confirmed date: April 8, 2027]
What it is: Joint congress of the International Osteoporosis Foundation (IOF) and ESCEO, April 8–11, 2027, at the VIECON Center in Vienna.
What problem it solves: It brings together data on secondary prevention of fragility fractures, fracture liaison services and treatments for osteoporosis and osteoarthritis, a field where most patients with a hip or vertebral fracture still receive no osteoporosis treatment after discharge.
Critical take: Strong pharmaceutical industry presence; results presented are often preliminary and not all apply to lower-resource health systems.
https://www.wco-iof-esceo.org/sites/wco_27/promotional/2027/WCO27-affiche.pdf

### 5. Intra-articular amniotic allograft (ReNu, azimplacel) for knee osteoarthritis pain
[Pending regulatory decision] [USA] [Confirmed date: April 24, 2027]
What it is: A cryopreserved amniotic suspension allograft given as a single intra-articular injection, versus saline, for pain in symptomatic knee osteoarthritis; the FDA accepted the biologics license application (BLA), as announced by Organogenesis on July 6, 2026. The second phase 3 trial (n=594) missed its primary endpoint, WOMAC pain reduction at 6 months (difference −0.51; one-sided p=0.0393 against a 0.023 threshold); the first phase 3 was positive (−0.72; one-sided p=0.0177), and the company supports the filing with a combined analysis of both.
What problem it solves: For knee osteoarthritis pain that does not respond to exercise, weight loss and analgesics, available injections (corticosteroids, hyaluronic acid) give modest or short-lived relief, and many "regenerative" products are used today without approval or solid evidence.
Critical take: Company sponsorship, one phase 3 negative on its primary endpoint, a saline comparator (which produces a large placebo effect in osteoarthritis) and small absolute WOMAC differences; approval would not validate other amniotic tissue products marketed without trials.
https://organogenesis.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/organogenesis-announces-fda-acceptance-biologics-license
https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/09/25/3156738/0/en/Organogenesis-Provides-Update-on-Second-Phase-3-ReNu-Study.html
